About the creator

Maritime Empire is made by one person: Rory Ferrick.

Who I am

I grew up in Menlo Park, California, a few miles from the Port of Redwood City and an hour from the container cranes of Oakland, and I am finishing a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science at Miami University in Oxford, Ohio, graduating in December 2026. Maritime Empire started as a personal project between classes and grew into the game you see on this site: a 2D shipping simulation of the whole world, its ports, its weather and the companies that move goods across it. I build it myself, from the economy and the routing to the art and the sound, and it is coming to Steam.

What I work on

I like systems that have to hold together at scale. My senior design project is a quantum computing emulator, which is a very different kind of simulation from a shipping game but asks the same question: how do you model something enormous faithfully enough that the result means something, and fast enough that someone can use it? Before that I wrote a ray tracer in C++ with lighting, shadows, reflections and anti-aliasing, and a Java application that works with a mining database through SQL. Those three projects, graphics, data and simulation, are the three legs this game stands on.

Professionally I am drawn to global supply chains and to agriculture. The world runs on ships nobody thinks about: grain from the Black Sea, ore from Brazil, cars from Korea, bananas from Ecuador. Working out how a port's stockpile, a producer's output and a shipping line's fleet fit together, and then making that into something you can play, is the most interesting problem I have found. Farms, livestock, fresh produce and the land side of the supply chain are in the game for the same reason, and I expect to keep working in that space after I graduate.

Away from the keyboard

Sport comes first. I played football for ten seasons, from Pop Warner through high school varsity, and baseball from Little League to varsity, with a season of golf and a season of lacrosse along the way. I follow football and baseball closely and I love car racing, which has given me an unreasonable appreciation for pit-stop timing that you may notice in how tugs and port stops work in the game.

I spent a semester at St Andrews College in Dublin in 2020, and in 2024 I completed a 28-day wilderness leadership expedition with the National Outdoor Leadership School: navigation, mountaineering, first aid and a lot of time learning how a small group gets through hard country together. Summers I have worked at the Stanford golf course, in parking operations for Tesla's Palo Alto workforce, and as a teacher's aid in a fifth-grade classroom.
