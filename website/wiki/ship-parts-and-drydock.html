Ship parts and drydock

Added or changed on 22 October 2026

  • Every model has a parts list from the catalogue by its tier and category, each a real part (main engine, propeller, hull plating, shaft, turbocharger, reefer plugs, cargo pumps, cryogenic containment, trawl winches, azimuth pods, stabilisers...) in a group (hull, propulsion, engine, electrics, deck gear, systems) with a wear rate and a weight. Each ship keeps a condition per part; its maintenance figure is the parts' weighted mean. Changes to maintenance (wear at sea, storms, breakdowns, repairs) are spread over the parts when next looked at: wear by rate x weight, so electrics and turbochargers go first and plating last.
  • The parts move the ship: a fouled hull burns up to 15% more fuel, worn propulsion costs up to 10% of speed, a tired engine or electrics raise breakdown odds up to 50%, worn deck gear lengthens port stops up to 20%. Ordinary port stops put right only the minor parts; the major ones (engine, propeller, shaft, plating, bow, cryogenic tanks, pods...) only in drydock, so a ship's maintenance slips below 100% between drydocks.
  • Drydock: at any of the shipyard ports or a hub of the company's with the Drydock facility. The ship must be in port with an empty hold. Cost, paid when the work starts: the repairs at the ship's repair cost a point (a quarter less at its own dock), the yard's fee (2% of list price; none at its own dock) and the upgrades. An order can be cancelled while the ship is on its way.
  • Upgrades: one per part where the catalogue has one (high-efficiency propeller, foul-release coating, electronic fuel injection, shaft generator, automated mooring, optimised bow, deepwell pumps, boil-off gas burning, CO2 refrigeration, wide quarter ramp, boom unloader, pod bearing refit, recovery turbine...), each a share of the ship's list price with factors on fuel, speed, breakdowns or port time, kept on the ship. Better ones come with technology (M5).
