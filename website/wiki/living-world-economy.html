Wiki / The world

Living world economy

Added or changed on 22 October 2026

  • The world's markets are trade zones: a country, or a big country's coastline zone, 151 in all, each with a stockpile and a price for every good (151 goods: every commodity a ship line carries). People live in 891 places; an inland city buys and sells through its serving ports' zones. A place's people split into lower, middle and upper strata by its country's GDP a head, each stratum with its wants of 15 needs (needs: staples, protein, clothing, fuel, medicine, produce, treats, household, electronics, cars, cosmetics, leisure, travel, delicacies, valuables; any good of a need serves it and the cheapest mix is bought first), lifted by the country's wealth; industry uses goods by drivers (users: make:<good> for recipe inputs, gdp, people, farming).
  • Every calendar day each zone's people buy, its industry takes its inputs, its plants make, and zones of one country even out by land. People grow at their country's real 2016 rate and drift from the countryside into town (people); needs follow every 30 days.
  • Producers: every country's output of every good is shared among plants at its ports (and, for farming, forestry and manufacturing, its inland cities) weighted to where that industry really is, about 14,000 plants, grouped into about 670 companies by country and trade, with 30 global companies holding many of the biggest plants and the vehicle and boat makers as companies of their own; names made up by country and trade. Made again from the save's seed on loading.
  • A dispatch's lots between two zones on one line sail as one voyage of the line's biggest ships, as many as the hold units take ({firm, model, ships, goods, from, to, start, arrive, free}): the goods leave the source's stockpile at once and arrive at the model's speed, the ships free again after the trip home, empty (the homeward legs are kept too, {free, line, hold, from, to, model, ships, firm, arrive}, so the map can draw them). A ship on a firm's books stands for CONVOY (10) hulls' worth of hold, so the world's fleet counts like the real one (about 50,000 ships in 2016) while the goods move in the game's hold units. On the 4x calendar a voyage takes weeks to months (Asia to Europe about 110 days), so zones order months ahead. Firms earn the margin less the freight. Voyages at sea (about 16,000 outbound and 5,000 homeward records) are kept in order of arrival and saved compactly; they're what the map draws as ambient traffic (below). The game is saved once it's there. After a year with the firms about four fifths of every zone's prices sit within 0.6x to 1.25x of base; the rest are mostly micro-markets (Bermuda, Greenland, Palau, the Faroes...) at the cap and gluts at tiny producers of niche goods at the floor.
  • Ambient traffic: the firms' voyages and homeward legs drawn as ships where the player looks. Where a voyage is follows from its times: as far along the lane between its ports as the play time is between its start and arrival. A record runs between its zones' main ports; its ghost sails between ports of those zones picked by the record (the main port likeliest), so a zone's traffic spreads along its coast. Settings > General > Other companies' ships: None, Light (50), Busy (150, the default) or Heavy (400) ghosts at once. Clicking a ghost opens its card: the firm and its home, the ship class and how many are on this run, the ports, the cargo (or in ballast), what it's doing and when it's due.
  • Producers on the map (2026-10-22): a port's producers' plants are drawn behind its town as an industrial estate, each the warehouse art with its good's band and its owner's colour mark, lit at night like a hub's industry. Clicking a plot opens its card: the works, its owner, where from and how many plants they have, what it makes a year, and the market it sells into with the price there now. The port popup says how many producers' plants are there and names the biggest.
  • Selling locally (2026-10-22): every plant of the player's (an industry site, a farm plot, a maker's factory, an energy building) has a "Sell locally" switch on its page. Off, output waits in the plant's store for the company's ships as before. A plant selling locally isn't capped by its store.
  • Intelligence page: Countries, Companies (the shipping firms by ships, at sea, earned and carried, a firm's card with its fleet by model and home port; the producers by plants and output a year, a producer's card listing its plants with buttons to them on the map), Markets (as the Markets page was: a good's cheapest ports to buy, dearest to sell and best trades, plus every zone's price of it as x its base with days of cover, stock, use and output a day and a year's sparkline, each a click to its chart), Charts and News (the headlines by kind beside the player's own activity log).
  • Backstory starts: on the New Company page every recommended port of the company type (its excellent, great, fair and hard starts and the Great Lakes: about 79 a type) has a start, an archetype from the 16 in the data picked by the port (the same every game; the archetypes dealt round the type's ports in a shuffled order so each turns up) and told in three or four sentences assembled from its parts (the founder's full name the first time, the first name after; {surname}, {port}, {country}, {money}, {debt}, {stake}, {ship}, {backer}, {firm} the biggest paper firm of the home country, and the first trait's story line). A start sets the money (scaled by the port's kind: an excellent port's story is poorer, a hard one's richer), any debt (the company's loan) or backers, granted ships, standing and the CEO's traits. A Custom start switch gives the port with - Hover cards: CK3-style nested tooltips. The card stays while the mouse is over it or its source, and lines inside it may be attached in turn, opening deeper cards beside them, the deeper closing as the mouse leaves. Attached to the port popup's market cells and the Intelligence Markets table's prices.
  • The Maritime Gazette: the newspaper, an issue a calendar week laid out from the news over the map: masthead and dateline with Earlier/Later, the week's lead story (the tightest-markets digest, else the first market move) with a paragraph, columns for Markets, Shipping and The world, and the company's own notes (the activity log's good and bad entries that week) down the side. Opened from the ticker's Gazette button and the News tab.
  • Milestones: the first facility, every facility at one hub.
  • Professions: a zone's working people by trade as shares, from its plants (each plant's yearly output in US its trade's profession: farmers, fishers, miners, factory hands), its ports' sizes (dockers and sailors) and its strata (clerks from the middle, traders from the upper), shown as a stacked bar with the shares under it on the country card, the city card and the port popup. They'll drive crew pools (M4) and events.
  • Multiplayer and the living world (2026-10-22): a joined game gets the world, the firms and the news whole on join (in the save's form). The joined game prunes voyages by time and draws its own ambient traffic from them; its world never steps.
  • Tutorial pacing and milestones (2026-10-22): the tutorial's tips wait for the player's actions rather than a timer: Finding better trades ends when a port popup is opened, What's behind a price when a price card is hovered, The Intelligence page when it's opened, Grow your fleet after three sales, Breakdowns once a ship is worn, and new tips for the competition's ships, the Gazette, the CEO's card and selling locally. Ten new badges in Achievements: Shipowner, Sold at the gate, Bricks and mortar, Full service, Front page, Market insight, The competition, Well informed, Clean slate and Globetrotter (trade in 10 countries).
