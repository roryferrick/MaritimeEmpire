Living firms

Added or changed on 29 October 2026

  • The 200 paper firms live. Each month a firm with the cash for a few ships (two, three or six ships' worth by its CEO's leaning) and no falling trend orders its favourite model from a yard with a slot, delivered onto its books after the yard's months; a firm in the red (cash under a tenth of its fleet's value) and falling for six months sells a ship at 60% of list; one whose cash stays under half its fleet's value in the red for three months goes bankrupt: up to six of its ships go to the second-hand market at half the usual price (a card gives the player first look), stakes in it are worthless, its name retires (it stays in the list as dead: no voyages, tenders, poaching or listings), and a new small firm is founded in its country with a ship or two, so the world stays near 200. Headlines for orders of big ships, deliveries, sales, bankruptcies and foundings.
  • Mergers: a firm with cash above 30% of its fleet's value buys a struggling firm of its country or line worth under 60% of its own; a small target (under a quarter) fetches 2x its value, a bigger one 1.3x, never under its ships' worth. A stubborn leadership (about a quarter of firms, cautious and loyal ones more often) will not sell for less than 5x, which no one pays: the press reports the rebuff. The target's fleet and voyages pass to the buyer; a player's stake in the target is sold at the deal's price or converted into the buyer by card (other companies' stakes convert).
  • Leanings: Expansionist orders early and overreaches; Cautious hoards cash; Cutthroat underbids tenders by 5%, poaches twice as often and holds grudges; Loyal keeps its word. About 4% of firms a year get a new CEO, and a new leaning.
  • Agreements: an alliance (neither bids against the other on tenders, neither poaches the other's people, the ally's home country thinks 10 points better of you; leaving it, or poaching from an ally, costs 10 standing with every country and a grudge), a pool of one trade group (each month you take your capacity's share of the firm's earnings in that group and pay it its share of yours), and a feud (both underbid each other by 10% on tenders, the rival poaches twice as often, your standing with its country slips a point a month and its cash half a percent; it sues for peace after a year when the feud is costing it, at most once in six months). Firms weigh your standing with them, their chief's leaning (loyal firms warm, cutthroat ones cold) and your stake before accepting; a loyal firm that likes you may propose an alliance, a cutthroat one sore at you may declare a feud, each by card.
