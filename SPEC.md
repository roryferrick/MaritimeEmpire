## Tech
- Engine: Godot 4 (standard build, GDScript). Target: Windows / Steam, mouse input, landscape only.
- Version control: Git. Keep game data (ports, ship models) in data files, not hard-coded.
- Placeholder visuals only (rectangles, circles, default Godot buttons). Structure UI so custom art can be swapped in later via a single Theme resource.
- Silent (no audio) for beta.
- Drawing budget (2026-10-12): what only moves with the globe is kept off the per-frame path. The fleet's route lanes are one mesh on the globe (LanePainter.build_mesh, scripts/ui/lanes.gdshader: each vertex carries its point's longitude and latitude and the way the line runs there, mitred at the corners like the native pass (the bearing of the bisector of the stretches in and out, and how far the corner reaches), and is put on screen by the view's basis, so a sharp turn never twists a piece of the line out of view; points on top of each other are dropped; LanePainter.build_mitred_mesh, which an older library lacks: the native pass draws the lanes then), rebuilt only when the lanes change; lanes whose ports' marinas show (their harbour curves are worked out on screen) stay with the native pass, and the split holds while zooming. Labels come from one atlas (LabelAtlas), the decor from one atlas with its cells rescanned every 4 moving frames (DecorSim rescan), far-off ships as one batch of dots from the native fleet pass, towns as native meshes (TownMesh), a marina's steady lights and bollards as batches. Lanes are laid out for the passes (their points, miles and box) over the frames after one that wanted them, never in a frame that draws. Port layouts, port dot styles, sound file paths and lengths, owned counts and tug ports are cached by what they depend on. Measured with a probe scene (see the performance notes in the project memory): idle 8-10 ms at every zoom, panning 9-16 ms, on save_3 at 1920x1080.

## Data & tools
- Game data lives in data/: ports.json (ports), ship_models.json (models, prices, speeds, ranges, limits, map look), ship_names.json (suggested ship names), game_config.json (starting money, XP and levels, autosave interval, initial map zoom), markets.json (commodities, regions and prices), canals.json (canals and locks).
- Generated data: data/world_map.res (land, coastline, lakes, rivers, borders, country names, sea depth bands), data/land_colors.webp (the land's real colors) and data/sea_lanes.res (a lane and distance for every pair of ports). Both are built by tools/build_map_data.gd from Natural Earth public-domain downloads kept in tools/source_data/ (not in git). The map's triangles are saved split to at most 2 degrees a side (Geo.tessellate_map()), so they curve round the globe without the game splitting them as it starts.
- After changing ports.json, rerun the tool (about 35-40 minutes; add `-- --lanes-only` to skip rebuilding the map art, or `-- --map-only` to rebuild only the map art after changing its source data). Source data in tools/source_data/: ne_10m_land, ne_50m_admin_0_countries, ne_10m_lakes, ne_10m_rivers_lake_centerlines , the 12 ne_10m_bathymetry_* depth files, ne_10m_geography_marine_polys and ne_10m_geography_regions_polys (GeoJSON), and Natural Earth II 1:50m with shaded relief (NE2_50M_SR, converted from its TIFF to NE2_50M_SR.png; `-- --map-only` rebuilds the map art in about 1.5 minutes):
  `Godot --headless --path . -s tools/build_map_data.gd`
- Narrow straits and river approaches the pathfinding grid is too coarse to see are listed in the tool (CHANNELS) and carved as water; add to that list if a new port ends up with odd routes. Canals with locks are in data/canals.json: the tool carves each canal's centerline and lays it exactly into every lane that crosses it. A lane crosses a canal if it crosses the canal's divide (a line along the land between the two seas) an odd number of times; an even number is a stray detour over the isthmus and back, which is cut out. After changing only canals.json, `-- --canals-only` re-lays the canals into the existing lanes in seconds (3,881 lanes go through Panama).
- How lanes are found: a fast plan on a coarse graph, then fitted to the fine 0.1-degree grid. Each coarse cell (0.5 degrees) is split into its separate pieces of water, linked only where the water really connects, so a plan never cuts across a thin strip of land (the Kra Isthmus, Shikoku's Bungo Channel...) and then has to double back. Turning points a great circle can skip clear of the coast are dropped, so long ocean legs are one smooth arc; every arc is checked cell by cell. The Oresund is carved (Copenhagen and the Baltic are reached through it, not round through the Great Belt).
- Lanes have no sharp corners: every turning point is rounded into a curve (a circular arc on the map, 60 nm across where there's room, using at most half of each leg so neighbouring curves join smoothly). A curve that would cross land is tightened, and the corner kept only if no curve fits. Canal centerlines are laid in exactly, so their own bends stay. Ships' headings also ease round any turning point (over about 0.3 map degrees either side), so they swing round bends instead of snapping. `-- --canals-only` doesn't round corners (it has no land grid); a full `--lanes-only` run does.
- Checking lanes: `-- --lanes-only --audit` lists lanes with a pointless detour (two points a straight line over open water could join saving at least 100 nm and 30% of the way), the worst, and where they cluster, into build/lane_audit.tsv (`--from <file>` audits another lanes file). `-- --lanes-only --pairs PUS-YOK,DXB-HKG` or `--ports CPH,GDN` re-routes just those lanes (or every lane to or from those ports) into the existing sea_lanes.res in about half a minute plus the routing. After any lanes run, `-- --channels-only` lays the ports' approach channels into the lanes again (a lanes run doesn't).
- Saves record port and model ids, so renaming models or adding ports keeps existing saves working; removing or renaming a port id does not.

## Economy & time
- Starting money: $2,500,000. Saves from before trading can't be loaded.
- NOT an idle game (for beta): time only passes while the game is open, at a constant speed, or 2x or 4x with fast forward (see Top bar).
- On quit, the game saves and the world freezes. On Continue, everything resumes exactly where it left off. Ships do not move and no money is earned while the game is closed.
- Ships trade (see Trading): they buy a commodity at one port and sell it at the next. Profit is the price difference, less fuel, repairs, canal tolls, port fees and crew wages.
- Port fees (game_config port_fee): a delivery sold at a port where the company has no HQ or hub pays 10% of the trade's margin (what the cargo sold for, less what it cost; nothing on a trade at a loss). The sale's log line shows it ("port fee $1,200"), the route screen's leg preview allows for it, and the Finances tab totals it as "Port fees" (the ship table puts it with the tolls).
- Crew wages (game_config crew; GameState._check_payday()): every ship has captains and crew by its tier (ship_models.json tier: tier 1 1 captain and 2 crew, tier 2 1 and 10, tier 3 2 and 30, tier 4 3 and 120, tier 5 4 and 320; service ships by their size: tugs tier 1, the Buffalo 2, the Mammoth and Diesel Icebreaker 3, the Nuclear Icebreaker 4). Every two calendar weeks (about 17 minutes of play) each ship that worked in that time (sailed, did a job or a stop in port) pays its full wages: $3,270 a captain and $1,090 a crew member (a Scooter $5,450, a Dominator $361,880); ships idle the whole time pay nothing. Calibrated on the median profitable route: about 20% of a Scooter's profit, 10% of a Feeder 100's, 5% of a Dominator's (crew-heavy livestock carriers more, big tankers and car carriers less). Each ship's officers are characters made up when it's bought (random names and looks, like the CEO's; Ship.captains, saved; saves from before get the same ones every load): the first is its Captain, in command, the rest its Mates. The shop's cards list each model's officers ("Captain + 2 mates"), crew and wages; the log says each payday ("Payday: $16,350 in crew wages for 3 ships"); the Finances tab totals them as "Crew wages" and gives each ship a Wages column. Saves from before wages start their first period when loaded.
- Loans (game_config loans; GameState.take_loan(), repay_loan()): on the Finances page's Your company tab, under Keep in the bank, a Loan row shows what's owed with Borrow $1M and Pay back $1M buttons: up to $10M in $1M steps (paying back needs the money; the last step pays off what's left). Interest of 8% a year is charged on what's owed each crew payday (a period's share, $30,685 on $10M), logged and counted in Finances as "Loan interest" (part of operating profit). Saved with the company.
- Debt: wages are paid whatever the money, so they can take it below $0 (the top bar shows it in red). A company in debt runs its ships on credit, buying their fuel, repairs, full cargo loads and canal tolls whatever the money, so nothing stops and sales pay the debt off; but it can buy nothing else (ships, upgrades, hub points) until it's back above $0.
- Every delivery (when unloading finishes) adds its units to the company's delivered totals, by commodity (shown on the Finances screen).
- Ships buy their own fuel at each port's local price, which follows the oil price there (cheapest in the Gulf, most expensive on remote islands); on average it's half the old $2.40. Repair costs are half what they were.
- Endless sandbox — no win condition.

## Trading
- 20 commodities (data/markets.json): Toys, Clothing, Furniture and Coffee (container ships); Oil and Gas (tankers); Iron ore, Coal and Gold ore (ore carriers); Wheat, Corn, Soybeans, Rice and Barley (grain carriers); Cattle, Sheep and Pigs (livestock carriers); Cars, Trucks and Boats (vehicle carriers). Every ship picks whichever of its goods pays best at each stop. Saves from before the grain, livestock and vehicle goods were split keep them as wheat, cattle and cars (GameData.current_commodity()).
- Their markets follow who really grows, raises or builds them: wheat cheap on the Canadian and US prairies' ports, the Black Sea, Argentina and Australia, dear in the Middle East and Africa; corn from the US Gulf and Great Lakes, Brazil and Argentina; soybeans from Brazil, Argentina and the US, dearest in China; rice from Vietnam, Thailand, Myanmar, Pakistan and India, dearest in West Africa and the Middle East; barley from Australia, Canada and the Black Sea, dearest in the Middle East; cattle from Argentina, Uruguay, Brazil, Queensland and the Top End; sheep from Western and South Australia, New Zealand and the Horn of Africa, dearest in the Middle East; pigs from Denmark, the Netherlands and Spain, dearest in East Asia; cars from Japan, Korea, Germany and Detroit; trucks from Gothenburg, Germany, Detroit, Japan and China; boats from Italy, Florida, Taiwan and the Netherlands, dearest in the Middle East and Africa.
- Livestock and vehicle carriers show what they carry (tools/make_cargo_skins.gd, art/ships/<line>_cargo_<good>.svg): brown and white cattle, cream sheep with dark faces or pink pigs in the pens; cars, trucks (a cab and a long box trailer) or white boats with a coloured stripe on the decks.
- Every port buys and sells everything. Prices come from 17 regions (0.5x where a good is produced to 2x where it's scarce: toys cheap in China, oil and gas in the Gulf, gold at Tema, Durban, Callao, Magadan, Port Moresby and Juneau, iron ore on the Great Lakes, in Brazil and Australia, vehicles in Japan, Korea, Germany and Detroit...), evened out between nearby ports (as competing traders would, over about 1,200 nm) so prices build up with distance, with a gentle local variation (up to 16%, varying over thousands of miles) and a coastal variation (typically up to about 3% either way, varying over about 1,600 nm, so neighbouring coasts differ a little and a Scooter can find a margin on most coasts) and a slow drift (up to 5% over about 20 minutes, neighbours drifting together). Buying costs 5% more than selling at the same port. Every commodity's base price was halved on 2026-10-08 (a container of toys is $2,000, a ton of oil $165, a car $4,500), so every buy and sell price is half what it was; fuel follows the oil price, so it halved too, while XP and market impact per load were kept the same (markets.json xp_ref_price and impact_ref_value halved with them). Your own trades nudge prices by up to 1% (for a very large load), recovering within a couple of minutes. Island groups (Hawaii, Japan, Fiji–Tonga–Samoa, the Azores and Madeira; markets.json island_markets) get small local gaps on top, so inter-island hops can pay: imports land cheaper at the main port (Honolulu, Suva, Pago Pago, Ponta Delgada) and cost more on the outer islands, and local products are cheaper where they are made (Kona coffee on the Big Island, Kobe coffee and Osaka clothing, Fiji gold); each group has at least one profitable container trade and one ore trade.
- Halfway through each stop a ship sells its cargo, then buys the most profitable cargo it can carry to its next stop (after the canal tolls on the way): a full hold (less what a canal makes it leave behind), or as much as the money allows, always keeping enough for the whole fleet to fill its fuel tanks. Nothing if no cargo makes a profit (it sails empty).
- Canal tolls are a share of the cargo's value at the destination (none for an empty ship). The rough-seas bonus (+50%), the upper-lakes bonus (+20%) and a hub's Sale prices upgrade add to a profitable trade's profit.
- Balance: a good route earns about what the same ship earned under the old flat pay, the best about twice that; short hops earn a little, long hauls between the right regions a lot.
- Markets tab: pick a commodity to see the cheapest ports to buy it, the most expensive to sell, and the best trades anywhere and within 800 nm (port names go to the map). The port popup shows every commodity's buy and sell price there (green cheap, red expensive). The World map's Prices picker has two columns: Price boxes, a ship line's prices in a box over each port (see the map's top right, below), and Port colors, every commodity grouped by its ship line with the line's swatch (containers orange, tankers teal, ore grey, grain gold, livestock pink, vehicles blue: the line's middle ship's map color, brightened a little), each seafood family with a little drawing of one of its species instead (fisheries.json families' icon: red king crab, snow crab, Dungeness, blue crab, stone crab, brown crab, blue swimmer crab, red crab, bluefin tuna, blue marlin, chinook salmon, Atlantic cod, halibut, Atlantic herring, Atlantic mackerel, mahi-mahi; the seafood line itself a tuna), on the menu and on the button: picking one colors every port by its price, green where it's cheap, white about its world average, red where it's dear (full color at 1.4 times cheaper or dearer: MapView.price_commodity, PORT_PRICE_SPAN), looked at again every second as prices drift; a port grown into its marina gets a ring in its color round its jetty instead, and by night its town's glow takes the color. The menu opens lined up with the button's right edge, and the chosen line's or commodity's name is shown in its color on the button. The route screen shows what each leg would load and its expected profit, or, for a leg with nothing worth carrying, that it sails empty and about what that costs in fuel and repairs (in red: "nothing to trade: sails empty, about -$184 in fuel and repairs"), at the arrival port's prices less any hub discount there.

## Fuel, maintenance and port stops
- Every ship has a fuel tank and a maintenance level (0–100%). Both start full when the ship is bought.
- Fuel burns at a fixed rate per second at sea, whatever the ship's speed.
- Maintenance drops 1% per minute while a running ship is at sea (the Dominator and Mammoth: 0.5% per minute). It doesn't drop while docked or while paused.
- Speed = top speed × maintenance, so a ship at 0% stops dead.
- Ships keep their speed at sea right up to the harbour mouth and away from it (game_config harbor_speed start 1.0: the old easing in and out at sea is off). They get going and slow down inside their marina instead (see Marinas). Near a port a ship sails a curve between its lane and the harbour (map_view _harbour_pose()): leaving, from just outside the harbour mouth (or its spot at the anchorage) round onto its lane; arriving, off its lane and round to the mouth heading in, swinging out to meet the entrance head on, then on into the marina without stopping. The curve takes 3 times the harbour's distance from the port of lane (at least 2 nm; more if the curve is longer, so a ship is never faster than at sea; the two ends share a short leg), gone along evenly, worked out once a leg.
- A slower, worn ship takes longer and so burns more fuel per leg. Tanks are sized so a ship leaving at 100% can just finish a leg of its full range (plus 1 s of spare fuel) while wearing on the way.
- Random breakdowns start at company level 6. Every 5 s, each ship at sea (never a recovery boat, and never inside a canal) rolls its own chance of losing 50 points of maintenance: 0.105% a roll at 100% maintenance, rising to 1.05% at 0% (worn ships break far more often). That's times the model's sturdiness (breakdown_factor: 1.0 for a Scooter down to 0.59 for a Dominator or Supertanker), times its age (+10% for every hour it has spent at sea, up to double), less its durability skill. A new Scooter at 100% breaks down about once every 80 minutes at sea. At 0% it's lost at sea. A ship that's slowed down can also run out of fuel before it arrives.
- Port stop: after arriving, a ship docks for a set time: Scooter 20 5 s, Feeder 100 10 s, Feedermax 200 15 s, Trans Atlantic 30 s, Dominator 1 min.
  - Cargo unloads over the first half of the stop (the cargo bar drains), and the ship is paid when unloading finishes. Cargo loads over the second half (the bar refills).
  - With auto-repair on, maintenance refills to 100% over the first 5 s, paid as it goes. With auto-refuel on, the tank refills over the next 5 s. The Scooter 20 does each in 2.5 s to fit its 5 s stop.
  - The ship can leave when the stop ends.
- Docks and berths (game_config docks; scripts/sim/docks.gd): a cargo ship's stop needs a berth. Every port has berths by its size band, 6 at a Small port, 8 at a Medium, 10 at a Large, 14 at a Giant, and 14 at a port with any company's HQ or hub, one fewer piers than berths each side (the first berth each side lies along the first pier's seaward face, open to the outer harbour; the last along the land end); every pier is as long as the longest ship the port takes, so any ship the port takes fits any of its berths (kind "L" in the config; a Small port's "s" berths take only the smaller ships, tiers 1-3, as before). Ships queue first come first served ("2nd in line" counts the ships waiting ahead). Berths are shared by every company's ships (in multiplayer too).
  - Coming alongside and pulling out take as long as the way at the ship's own sea speed (GameState._berth_seconds(), worked out by the map's berth_route_seconds() from its marina, its spot at the anchorage and the ship's length; saved as Ship.berth_duration), plus docks slide_s (1.5 s) sliding the last bit onto the pier on its thrusters, so ships never move faster than at sea, at any size of port. Coming in it sails steadily from where it is (its spot at the anchorage, or the mouth, arriving from sea) in through the entrance on its right-hand side, through the outer harbour and down the channel on the side across from its pier (a quarter of the channel's width off its middle, room to come round), round into its slot's mouth bow first, 0.7 of a length before it, and straight on in along the slot's middle line, slowing over its last length (map_view _slot_approach()); pulling out, it backs straight out of its slot stern first at half its speed until its bow is clear of the pier tips, turns round there in the channel on its thrusters (2.5 s; _unberth_plan(), all counted in its time), then steadily out through the entrance on its right-hand side to sea (or to its spot at the anchorage, slowing to anchor there), curving onto its lane and always heading the way it moves. Turning is smooth throughout: its heading eases round every bend rather than stepping, and it slows for any bend tighter than its own length across (never below 15% of its speed; the time it takes counts this too). Weighing anchor (or a tug leaving its moorings) a ship sets off the way it faces, as a boat does, and where that isn't the way it's going comes round on a turning circle 0.8 of its length across, through poses round the circle a quarter turn apart at most (map_view _loosen()); coming to anchor it does the same in reverse, arriving heading into the wind. The only sideways movement in port is the last slide onto the pier, and backing out of a slot. A lane that comes in to a port from behind or beside its jetty (along the coast) rounds the jetty's end, 1.5 of its radius out, to the mouth (_round_jetty(); the ships' own curves do the same), so nothing sails through the marina: from behind the marina (level with the jetty's end or nearer) it first runs along past the marina's wall; from further along the coast it makes straight for the jetty's end (turning out by the wall would loop it out to sea and back, as Shanghai's lanes from the north did); and where a plain curve to the mouth clears the jetty anyway it isn't rounded at all. Without a map (in_s 6 and out_s 5) the old fixed times stand.
  - Marinas (scripts/ui/map_view.gd _draw_marina(), _marina_layout()): zoomed in, each port's dot grows into its marina once that's bigger on screen than the dot. It's a rectangular basin dug into the land behind the coast facing the sea (data/port_facing.json), open to the sea, with piers reaching in from its two side walls like a rib cage (half its berths each side, rounded down, the odd berth on the +y side; each as long as the longest ship the port takes, 0.6 dock units wide; the slot between one pier and the next is two ships' widths and 2.1 to spare, room for two ships side by side, each coming straight in), a channel between their tips 1.2 ship lengths wide (at least 4 widths) from the entrance to the land end, the first slot each side open to the outer harbour (one ship: a width and 1.4 to spare before the first pier), and behind the basin a stone quay 0.9 of a length deep (at least 8 units) for the HQ and industry. A ship moors alongside its pier, bow to the wall, tied to the bollards along it: the first each side along the first pier's seaward face, then two to each slot, one along the pier on its land side and one along the pier on its sea side (the last slot's land side is the basin's end wall), each backing straight out along its own lane to leave. The jetty is a breakwater curving out round the sea side from where the walls meet the coast, with the entrance in its middle, lit red and green; inside it, the outer harbour where ships come round. Outside the jetty's arms, off to either side (where that's water; clear of the lanes rounding the jetty and of the approach funnel), lie the rocks the birds perch on (3 at a Small port, 4 at a Medium, 5 at a Large or bigger; map_view ROCK_SPOTS, art/sea/rock_a-c.svg), each a ship's width or more across, foam round it. Behind the industry's half of the quay a pad of quay reaches 3 units further inland for the port's lighthouse, a round white tower with a red band seen from above (_draw_lighthouse(); lit at night, see Night and day). Sizes in dock units (1/75 of a degree): a Small port's basin 14 wide and 8 deep (jetty radius 8), a Medium's 20 by 16 (11), a Large's 26 by 19 (14), a Giant's or hub's 26 by 26 (14), their quays 8 deep. Ports a canal runs through (data/canals.json ports: Port Said and Port Tewfik on Suez, Colon and Balboa on Panama) have the canal coming in through a cut in the land end, straight on from the channel. An HQ's or hub's ring shows round its port only while its marina is too small to draw. On the quay behind the basin, on the side with its name (-y), stands its HQ building (map_view _draw_hq_building(), _hq_region()), seen from above, a rectangular block (no longer curved round the basin) which grows through 10 levels, one every 10 company levels (level 1 to start, 10 from company level 90), from the inland corner towards the basin: a little wooden hut with the company's flag, a wooden house with its color on the eaves, a stone lodge with an annex, a brick office with its sign and roof gear, concrete offices with planters and then a deck with sunshades in its color, glass offices adding a lawn, solar panels and a pool, and at level 10 a tech campus filling its half of the quay, with a roof terrace and a helicopter pad. Taller buildings cast longer shadows.
  - Towns (map_view _town(), _draw_town()): every port has its town round its marina, just to look at: blocks of buildings on a street grid behind the quay (inland of the lighthouse's pad) and along the shore either side of the basin, 4, 5, 7 or 9 rows deep by size band and the marina's width and more across, each block only where the map's land is (so a town hugs its coast), a cross street every few blocks, some blocks two cells long (halls and terraces; grey ribbed warehouses along the waterfront), parks between. Seen from above: walls with the roof inset, pitched roofs with a ridge (the sunny side lighter), flat roofs with a parapet and roof gear, East Asian roofs in tiers; a few tall buildings with long shadows (most in the Americas and East Asia); and a landmark on the waterfront in the local manner (a church tower, a minaret and dome, a pagoda, a skyscraper, onion domes, a stupa or a dome). The look is the port's market region's, or its country's where that tells (TOWN_STYLES: the Mediterranean's pale walls and terracotta; Northern Europe's painted wood and dark roofs; East Asia's and Japan's grey tiles; Southeast Asia's red tiles; South Asia's, the Middle East's and Africa's flat sand-colored roofs; the Caribbean's pastels; North America's grid of grey towers; South America's colonial colors; Oceania's metal roofs; Russia's and the Arctic's; Antarctica's huts). Laid out once per port by a seed of its id and drawn into its marina's item, once the map's land is sorted. Roads tie it together (_town() roads): a quay road behind the quay with a spine across the quay strip to the basin, a spur to the HQ and a cross road past the industry plots; an avenue inland through the middle of the town with a dashed line down it; a road along each wide street and each cross street, pale walkways along the narrow streets; and a shore road along each side of the basin. At night (and under storms) its windows light up in the style's warmth over a glow across the town, with street lamps along the quay road and the avenue (drawn as one run of dashes per town under the marina's transform, so a night costs about 2 ms at a hub, not 20; see Night and day). Neighbouring towns share the land between them: the bigger port's (by rank) is laid out first and the smaller one keeps off its ground (blocks, parks, roads, quay and basin, on a grid of 1.6 dock-unit cells: _town_ground()), so no block stands on another town's; the smaller town's roads stop at the edge and each is joined on to the bigger town's nearest road by a link road at whatever angle their grids meet (_join_neighbour_towns(), within 9 dock units, over land), so two towns grown together read as one. No tree or mountain stands on a town either: any land drawing whose picture would reach onto a town's ground is hidden once the town is laid out (_clear_decor_off_town(); the animals keep their spots).
  - Arriving, a ship queues for a berth, first come first served ("Waiting at anchor for a berth at Rome (2nd in line)"), lying at the anchorage while it waits; with one free it comes in ("Coming alongside at Rome"): in through the entrance, down the channel, round into its slot and the last slide onto the pier on its thrusters, and is tied to the bollards (bow and stern lines and two springs). Only then does its stop (unloading, repairs, refuelling, loading). When it's done it pulls out ("Pulling out of Rome for Tunis"): backing out of its slot, turning in the channel and out to sea. A ship that can't go yet (held, paused, no route, waiting for a full load, money for fuel, a storm, ice) stays at its pier while the port has at least 2 other berths free, else pulls out to the anchorage, freeing the berth, and waits there (topping up its fuel as it can); every 2 s of play a cargo ship lying at the anchorage with nothing to do (waiting, idle, or new) comes in to a pier if the port has more than 3 free (GameState._wake_waiting_ships()), so the anchorage is the overflow, not the rule. Tugs, icebreakers and recovery boats take no berth: tugs moor along the piers, the rest lie at the anchorage.
  - Anchorages (data/anchorages.json, built by tools/build_anchorages.tscn in about 45 s from the map; rerun after changing ports, ship sizes or the marinas): every port has two grids of anchor points offshore, nearest the port first (the sides before straight out in front; none in the funnel in front of the harbour mouth, where every ship in or out sails, none behind the port, and none in or near another port's marina or funnel), each point clear of land all the way round as far as the ship lying there could swing: one with room for the longest ship of all (the Mammoth), the other closer and denser for ships up to 9 dock units long. Points too near the port's marina as it is now are skipped. A ship dropping anchor takes the first free point out of the shipping lanes: one no lane the map draws (every company's routes, just as laid), nor any ship's way between a harbour mouth near it and such a lane (the curves in and out drawn with the lanes, worked out on the ground wherever the view is), passes within its swing of, with 3 nm to spare; only if every point is in a lane does it take the first free one. So ships at anchor wait between the lanes where they can (the anchorage is the overflow: a ship lying in port takes a slip at its moorings first, see Long-term moorings). Which points are in the lanes is worked out again whenever the routes sailed change; ships already lying there stay put. A ship at anchor lies with its bow at its anchor, swung round to face into the wind (see Weather); in a storm it swings and pitches about the anchor.
  - Berths, queues and the ships coming alongside or pulling out are saved; a ship from an older save that was mid-stop queues to finish it. In multiplayer the host's berths and the docking times are sent to joined games (protocol 3).
- Auto-repair, auto-refuel and auto-recovery are per-ship toggles in the ship popup, all on for new ships (and for saved ships from before a toggle existed).
- Ships buy cargo only with money beyond the fleet's fuel reserve: what it would cost to top up every ship's tank where it is (or, for a cargo ship at sea, where it's going, counting the fuel it will burn getting there, at its speed now). It's worked out again every 3 s of play, since with a big fleet it's costly. A ship already full in port reserves nothing, so big idle tanks (a Buffalo's 645,000 units) don't lock up the money.
- Full loads (the ship popup's Full toggle, on for new and saved cargo ships): a ship only buys a full hold (less anything a canal makes it leave behind), never a part load, and with an empty hold it waits in port until it can afford one ("Docked at Rome — waiting for money for a full load to Malta"). A leg where no cargo makes a profit is sailed empty straight away. Waiting isn't treated as a problem (no red dot or log message). With it off, a ship buys as much as the money allows and sails.
- A ship only leaves if it has enough fuel for the whole next leg (plus 1 s spare), allowing for the wear it will pick up on the way. So only a breakdown can leave it stranded at sea. Otherwise it is held in port (red dot, a toast, and the reason in its status): refuel is off, it's waiting for money, it's too worn to make the leg on a full tank (turn on repair), or the leg is beyond its range (for routes set before ranges shrank; assign a new one).
- Calling in: a cargo ship lying at the anchorage (waiting, idle, or new) loads, refuels and repairs only in port. Given a route (or able to sail again), it comes in for a stop (queuing for a berth, coming alongside, a full stop: repairs, fuel, loading) when there's a cargo to pick up for its first leg (with full loads on, once it can afford a full hold; until then it waits at anchor, saying so), or when it needs fuel for the leg or is under 95% maintenance and there's money for them. Otherwise (nothing to load, the fuel for the leg and 95% or better) it sails straight from the anchorage. A cargo already aboard stays aboard through such a stop.
- Money never goes below $0 but through crew wages (see Economy & time: Debt). Refills buy what the player can afford, and held ships keep buying as money comes in, until they have enough fuel (with auto-refuel on, until the tank is full or the money runs out).

## Lost ships and recovery boats
- A ship lost within reach of free tugs based at either end of its leg is towed in by them instead (see Tugs); otherwise a recovery boat goes, as below.
- A ship at sea that runs out of fuel or hits 0% maintenance is lost. It stops where it is, gets a red "!" above it on the map and a red dot on the Ships screen, and a toast says so.
- The Mammoth is a recovery boat ($10,000,000, at most 8). It's the biggest ship, as fast as a Trans Atlantic (6.35 nm/s), and the most expensive to run: 4,500 fuel units/s ($10,800/s) and $300,000 per 1% of repair.
- The Buffalo is a smaller recovery boat ($1,250,000, but only $150,000 for the company's first ever Buffalo; at most 8), the size of a Trans Atlantic and as fast. It can only carry Scooter 20s, Feeder 100s, Feedermax 200s and Coastal tankers, costs about a tenth as much to run (450 fuel units/s, $1,080/s, and $30,000 per 1% of repair), wears 1% per minute, and does jobs up to 8,000 nm.
- A lost ship's popup has a Send Recovery button. It shows the estimated cost (the recovery boat's fuel and repairs, including its trip home), which boat would go, where it would take the ship, and about how long. You choose whether it's worth it.
- The nearest free boat that can carry the ship goes (the cheaper one if two are as near): one that's docked, not refueling, and not on a job. Boats sailing to their base count as busy. A boat only sets off if it has the fuel and maintenance to finish the job.
- Auto-recovery ("Rescue" toggle in the ship popup, on for new ships): when the ship is lost, the cheapest capable free boat is sent automatically. If none is free, lost ships wait in a queue (longest-lost first) and the next free boat takes them.
- A ship at sea that no longer has the fuel or maintenance to reach port (usually after a breakdown) gets an amber "!" on the map, "won't make it!" in its status, and a toast.
- The Mammoth sails the sea lanes to the lost ship's position, through whichever end of the ship's leg is closer. It keeps the heading it came in on, driving on past the ship in line and slowing to a stop clear in front of it, its stern a little past the ship's end (3 s; "Pulling up ahead of Sea Otter"), and takes it aboard like a real semi-submersible heavy-lift ship (4.5 s): it ballasts down until its work deck is under water (the sea and ripples drawn over the deck, the wheelhouse still above), reverses under the ship, which floats in over its open stern, then deballasts and rises with the ship centered on deck. It picks ships up facing either way: one it came up behind floats in bow first and rides facing forward, one it met head on floats in stern first and rides facing aft ("Ballasting down to take Sea Otter aboard", "Floating Sea Otter onto its deck", "Rising with Sea Otter aboard"). Only if the port it's carrying the ship to is back the way it came does the pair spin round on the spot, about its middle (2 s; "Spinning round with Sea Otter aboard"); then it heads off along the lane. At the port it floats the ship off the same way (4.5 s: ballasts down, the ship slides off over the stern, it rises) before handing it over ("Floating Sea Otter off at Rome"). These times count in the job's estimate.
- It carries the ship to the nearer end of its leg. If the company has tugs based there, it only carries it to 90% of their longest reach out, floats it off at sea ("Golden Pipit brought Arctic Parakeet within reach of Suape's tugs") and sails on into port itself; the ship is lost again there on its leg, and the tugs fetch it in (at once if free, else when auto-recovery next finds them free). The popup's estimate says so ("Golden Pipit → 135 nm off Suape for its tugs"). The carried ship is drawn on the boat's open work deck, behind its middle, so the wheelhouse near the bow stays in view. Recovery boats are drawn longer than the ships they carry (Buffalo 13 px, Mammoth 20 px long at the opening zoom: ships are drawn at 0.35 of their map_size, half what they were).
  - At the ship's destination, it unloads and is paid as usual.
  - Back at its origin, it gets no pay. It docks (repair and refuel as its toggles say) and tries the leg again.
- The Mammoth then docks there (repair and refuel like any ship), sails home, and waits for the next job. Its bars show maintenance, fuel, and "Tow" (the ship it's carrying).
- Each recovery boat is based at the HQ or a hub, and returns there after a job. Boats are spread evenly across the HQ and hubs, model by model (each location gets its share; locations that already have the most keep any extra one). It rebalances when a hub is founded or a recovery boat is bought or sold: boats already at a location within its share stay, and the rest are re-based to the nearest location still short that a full tank can reach. A re-based boat sails there (paying its own fuel and repairs) once it's free. The Hubs screen lists the boats based at each location.
- Recovery boats have no route and no Assign Route or Pause buttons, and never break down at random. They pay their own fuel and repairs: those show on the recovery boat's own line in the Finances tab.

## Company and ship levels (XP)
- Every delivery (when unloading finishes) gives XP to the company and to the ship for the work done: units delivered × leg distance × 0.01, weighted by the commodity's base price against a container of toys (a ton of oil counts for less). It doesn't depend on the profit, so a trade at a loss still earns XP. Levelling is about twice as fast as before the trading update (company level costs, hub level costs and each model's first ship level all halved), so level 100 takes about 20 hours.
- The company levels from 1 to 100 (Level and an XP bar in the top bar; toast on level-up with what it unlocked). Each level's cost is set so it takes roughly 3 minutes at level 1, rising to about 47 minutes at level 99, of what a full fleet at that level earns. So bigger ships don't make levels fly by, and level 100 takes about 40 hours (about 1 hour to level 10, 11 hours to 50, 24 hours to 75).
- Ship slots are per model: each container ship and gas tanker can be owned 3 at a time when it unlocks, plus 1 more every 3 company levels, up to its max of 8 (so 15 levels after unlocking). The Scooter 20 and Feeder 100 gain a slot every 2 levels instead (full 10 levels after unlocking). Recovery boats start at 1 and gain 1 every 5 levels, up to 8. Level-up messages say which models got a slot ("+1 slot: Scooter 20, Feeder 100").
- Models unlock by company level: Scooter 20 at 1, Feeder 100 at 5, Buffalo at 6 (with breakdowns and storms), Feedermax 200 at 12, Coastal at 15, Trans Atlantic and Mammoth at 25, Aframax at 35, Dominator at 50, Supertanker at 60. Ships already owned are kept even if they're above the current slots or unlocks.
- Ships level from 0 to 30. A model's first level needs xp_first_level XP (Scooter 20: 100, Feeder 100: 1,250, Feedermax 200: 6,000, Trans Atlantic: 75,000, Dominator: 900,000; growing faster than the price, so small ships level fastest), and each level after costs 5% more.
- Each ship level gives a skill point, spent in the ship popup's Skills view on three paths of up to 10 levels each: Speed (+2% top speed per level), Durability (−5% wear and −5% chance of taking a random breakdown per level), and Gas efficiency (−3% fuel burn per level). Range on the route screen stays at the model's base range.
- Upgrades also cost money, each skill path priced separately: a path's 1st level costs 5% of the model's shop price, each next level 2.5% more (5%, 7.5% ... 27.5% for the 10th; game_config skill_costs), so a Scooter 20's first is $625 and a Dominator's $2,500,000; maxing all three paths costs 4.875x the price. The Skills view's buttons show the price ("+ $12.5M", "Max" when maxed), greyed out with the reason when it can't be afforded. Upgrades bought before costs came in stay free. The Finances totals list it as "Ship upgrades".
- Recovery boats level too, 0 to 30, with the same three skills. Each finished recovery (when the boat drops the ship off) gives the boat 1 XP per mile of the job (out to the lost ship and carrying it to port), and the company the XP the rescued ship's full hold would earn over the miles it was carried (a small bonus next to deliveries). First levels: Buffalo 400 XP, Mammoth 1,000 (a Buffalo job averages about 1,050 nm, a Mammoth one about 2,700 nm), so the first level takes about one job and level 30 about 20 hours of play. Their tiles show "Recovery · Lv 3".
- Saves from before XP get the XP their past income would have earned.

## Market regions
- The 17 market regions (markets.json regions) start locked, all but the home port's. On the map a locked region is shaded dark, its ports are small faint dots that can't be clicked (zoomed in, their marinas show like any others, and their names are shown dimmed), its land drawings aren't drawn, and the Markets tab leaves its ports out. No route can stop at its ports and no hub can be built there (route_error, build_hub_error: "Singapore is in Southeast Asia, which you haven't unlocked yet."), but ships may sail through it. Port growth messages for its ports aren't logged.
- A region's area on the globe: every point belongs to the region of its nearest port (scripts/ui/region_map.gd, worked out once on the GPU as the game starts).
- One more region can be unlocked every 5 company levels (game_config regions.every_company_levels; all 17 by level 80). The level-up toast says "a new region to unlock", the World tab gets a black dot, and an "Unlock a region" button (with the count, if more than one) heads the map's top-right panel. It opens the choice: the regions are outlined, every locked region has a button with its name at its middle, the locked region under the mouse (or button) lights up gold, and a banner at the bottom explains (Cancel or Esc closes it). Clicking a region or its button arms it ("Click again to unlock Southeast Asia"); clicking again unlocks it for good, with a log message.
- Saved per company (multiplayer: each player's own; joined players' unlocks go through the host). Saves from before regions unlock the home port's region and every region the company's hubs and ships use (routes, the ports they're at or sailing between, recovery bases).

## Headquarters and hubs
- The company starts with its headquarters (HQ) at its home port. It gets one more hub every 15 company levels (15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90: 7 locations in all). A hub is founded from any port's popup ("Build a hub here", click again to confirm) and is permanent.
- Each HQ/hub levels from 0 to 40 on the XP of every delivery unloaded at its port (the same XP the company gets), and of every full load shipped out of it by a ship that arrived empty (a one-way route out of the hub: the hub gets the XP the load will earn where it's delivered, when it's loaded; the company and ship still get theirs on delivery). Level L to L+1 costs (5 + 0.7 x L) minutes of a quarter of a full fleet's XP at the current company level, so a hub taking about a quarter of the fleet's deliveries maxes out in about 12.5 hours.
- Each level is an upgrade point, spent in the port popup on four paths of up to 10 points each. Each point also costs money: $2M for a path's first point, then $500k more for each point already in that path ($2.5M, $3M ... $6.5M for the 10th; $42.5M for a full path). The buttons show the price and grey out when you can't afford it; the Finances tab totals it as "Hub upgrades". They boost every ship docking there: Ship XP +5% per point (ship XP only, not company or hub XP), Fuel & repairs -3% price, Port stops -5% time (unloading, repairs and refueling all speed up), Pay +2% on deliveries unloaded there. The HQ's bonuses are 1.5x a hub's.
- Every delivery unloaded at an HQ or hub also earns the company extra XP, free with the hub's level: +10%, plus 10% more for every 10 hub levels (+20% at level 10 ... +50% at level 40). The HQ's is 1.5x (+15% up to +75%). The hub's own XP and the ship's aren't boosted by it. The Hubs cards and port popup show it, e.g. "Company XP +30% on deliveries here (+40% at level 30)".
- On the map, the HQ and hubs have a ring in the company color around their port (heavier for the HQ; gray on the Route Assignment map). A small red dot sits just up and to the right of any HQ or hub with an upgrade you can buy now. The Hubs tab shows a black dot while a hub has an upgrade you can buy (a point and the money) or a new hub can be founded.
- New ships launch at the HQ or any hub, chosen when buying; recovery boats are then re-based across the hubs (see Lost ships and recovery boats). Saves from before hubs get an HQ at their home port.
- Industry (game_config hubs.industry): every HQ and hub has room for 3 commodity buildings, on the quay behind the marina's basin, on the side across the channel from the HQ building (+y), in a row of equal square plots nearest the basin (MapView _industry_plots()). One plot opens at each of hub levels 10, 20 and 30. Each building is one commodity's (20, one each, with its own top-down art in art/industry: Toy factory, Textile mill, Furniture works, Coffee roastery, Oil refinery, Gas terminal, Steel mill, Coal power station, Gold refinery, Flour mill, Corn mill, Soybean crushing plant, Rice mill, Malting house, Cattle ranch, Wool mill, Pig farm, Car plant, Truck plant, Boatyard); a hub can't have two of the same, but the same one can be built at other hubs. Each costs a flat $15M (Finances "Hub upgrades") and can be pulled down (confirmed) for half back, freeing the plot. At its port, for its owner's ships only, its commodity costs 20% less to buy and sells for 20% more, and every delivery of it loaded or unloaded there earns 50% more company and ship XP (the HQ's are 1.5x: -30%, +30%, +75%; GameState.company_buy_price(), company_sell_price(), industry_xp_bonus(); ships' cargo choice and the route and ship popups' estimates use these prices). Built from the Hubs tab (each card's Industry section) or by clicking a plot on the map (an industry popup with the same section): an open plot shows "Build ($15M)", which opens a picker of every commodity's building (ones already there greyed); a built one shows its boosts and "Pull down". On the map an open, empty plot of this player's own hub is marked out with a dashed line; built ones show their art with a shadow, at any company's hub. In multiplayer they're host commands (build_industry, demolish_industry). Saved with each hub.

## World
- The whole Earth as a globe, seen from space (an orthographic view, north kept up), like Google Earth. Drag to turn it (the map follows the mouse; let go while moving and it keeps spinning, slowing to a stop), and zoom with the mouse wheel (easing in and out, about the point under the mouse), or on a trackpad by pinching (about the fingers) or swiping two fingers up and down like the wheel, or with the + and - keys, or the World map's + and − buttons at its bottom right, from the whole globe (90% of the view's height across) down to a close view of a harbor. The view can turn right over the poles (Antarctica's ice and the deep Arctic Ocean reach them). Around the globe is dark space with a soft blue glow of air at its edge; the globe darkens a little and fades into haze towards its edge.
- How it's drawn (scripts/ui/map_view.gd): the map data stays in Web Mercator map coordinates; the land art is wrapped round the sphere by a shader (scripts/ui/globe.gdshader), and everything else (ports, ships, lanes, labels, storms) is placed through the same view, so nothing round the far side shows. Ships' headings and the docks turn with the globe's north where they are. The biomes are cut to the land by a land mask drawn off screen (CanvasItem clip_children can't follow the shader). Sizes set in projected degrees (canal channels, locks, the land drawings' spacing) shrink towards the poles as the land does; ships and docks keep their size in the world. Piers show once a port's docks are 8 px out to the mouth.
- Keeping the map fast (zoomed in to the marinas, and dragging): each port's marina is drawn once into its own canvas item at the zoom it's drawn at and then only moved and turned with the view (drawn again only when the zoom changes, the port changes size or becomes a hub, or its HQ building grows); when the view moves, every layer is asked to draw again once, in that frame's _process (asked from input, they drew twice a frame); label text sizes are measured once, and labels, ports, land drawings and lanes' points are kept on the unit sphere (they never move) and looked up by cell of the globe near the view, so off-screen ones cost nothing (a long segment, open ocean between two points far apart, is drawn in pieces along the path the ships sail, straight in the map's projection, which bends with the globe zoomed out, so a ship's dot sits on its line: before, the straight screen line between the points left dots up to 30 px off at the widest zooms); the route lines' harbour curves are worked out once a zoom (and again after the view moves a degree) and kept on the globe; the land mask under the biomes is drawn again only when the view changes; birds' shapes are a bird a unit across, scaled as they're drawn, and their colors read once; what birds are where is kept for a game minute; sea animals' wander spots and the calendar year are worked out once. None of it changes how anything plays or looks. Dragging at marina zoom went from 70-90 ms a frame to about 8-10 ms (on the development machine).
- Map art: blue sea, sandy land with a darker coastline, thin country borders, lakes (drawn with a shoreline, each in a simplified version of its real color: clear blue for most fresh water, deeper blue for very deep clear lakes like Baikal, Tanganyika, Malawi, Titicaca and the upper Great Lakes, green for shallow plant-rich ones like Victoria, Erie and Winnipeg, muddy for Chad and Tonle Sap, turquoise for glacial and salt lakes like the Dead Sea, jade for Turkana, pink for red-algae ones like the Syvash, and salt-white for dry salt pans like Lake Eyre and inland Australia's; islands in them are land; plain gray on the route map), thin blue rivers (the biggest show zoomed out, smaller ones appear as you zoom in; visual only, ships don't sail them), and country names that appear as you zoom in (placeholder colors, all in the theme). - Every port has a size from 1 to 100 (game_config port_sizes), in four bands: Small 1–25, Medium 26–50, Large 51–75, Giant 76–100. Each save keeps its own sizes.
  - Starting sizes (ports.json size): within each market region (East Asia, the Mediterranean, the Great Lakes, Antarctica...), ports are banded by real container volume (the top ~15% Giant, the next ~25% Large, the next ~30% Medium, the rest Small; at least one of each per region: 34 Giant, 58 Large, 69 Medium, 66 Small) and spread by rank within the band (Shanghai 100, Hong Kong 63, Rome 29, Taipei 11).

Tuned so a mid-game company's busiest port (about $60M an hour through a size-50 port) climbs a band in about 15 hours. Growth is banked (up to 5 points) and applied at no more than 5 points an hour (a band in 5 hours), so late-game fleets grow their busy ports at that cap.
  - Shrinking: from company level 46, every port above 51 loses 0.83 points an hour (a band in about 30 hours), less whatever it grows, and never goes below 51 (a Large or Giant port stays at least Large). Small and Medium ports never shrink, and nothing shrinks before level 46.

New ships launch at any HQ or hub (but not a tier-4 or 5 ship at a Great Lakes one). A ship whose next port is too small is held ("assign a new route"), though with the size floor this can't happen in normal play.
  - Saves from before port sizes: every port a ship is at, sailing between or routed through is raised just enough for it (e.g. Taipei to 26 for a Trans Atlantic), so no ship is held.
  - On the map a port's dot grows smoothly with its size: 3 px radius at 1, 6 at 63 (as before), 9 at 100; docked ships ring just outside it and hub rings hug it.

## Panama Canal
- The canal is defined in data/canals.json: its centerline (Limón Bay → Gatún Locks → Gatún Lake → Culebra Cut → Pedro Miguel Locks → Miraflores Locks → Balboa), its locks, and its tuning. Every lane through it follows that centerline exactly.
- Three sets of locks, five chambers: Gatún (3, lifting ships from the Atlantic to Gatún Lake), Pedro Miguel (1) and Miraflores (1, lowering them to the Pacific), all out in the middle of the isthmus, clear of Colon's and Balboa's marinas (which are drawn many times real size). Chambers are drawn about 1.5 nm apart, several times real size, so they're visible at full zoom (like the ships).
- Each chamber has two lanes side by side, one per direction, and holds one ship per lane. A ship sails into a chamber and sits for 3 s while the water rises or falls, then sails on. If the next chamber is close (the rest of the same flight, or Pedro Miguel to Miraflores), it stays in its chamber until that one is free. Northbound and southbound ships never block each other.
- A ship coming up to a busy chamber waits in line behind it, 1.6 nm behind the ship ahead, first come first served (recovery boats too). No cap on the line.
- Engines are off in a chamber, in line and at the toll: no fuel burned, no wear. Nothing breaks down anywhere between the canal's entrance and exit. Fuel checks before departure only count sailing time, so they're unchanged.
- Toll: a cargo ship entering the canal pays 20% of the leg's base pay (before any hub Pay bonus). It's logged, counted in Finances ("Canal tolls", per ship and overall), and taken off the profit reported when the ship leaves the port at the end of that leg. A ship without the money waits at the entrance ("Waiting for toll money", a red dot on the Ships tab) and goes once it can pay. Recovery boats (and ships they carry) pay nothing.
- XP bonus: a delivery from a leg through the canal earns +50% XP for the company, the ship and the hub where it's unloaded, added to the other bonuses (a hub's +30% company XP becomes +80%).
- On the map the canal is always drawn as a wide water channel across the isthmus (as are all the canals), wider as you zoom in. From 120 px per degree its chambers appear: two lanes each, with gates at the ends, the water darkening or lightening as a ship inside is lowered or lifted. Ships in the canal keep to the right-hand lane. "Panama Canal" is labeled at mid zoom, and each lock's name when zoomed in close.
- Clicking a lock (once its chambers are drawn) opens a popup: for each direction, each chamber's ship ("Ever Bright, rising (2 s)", "coming in", "waiting to move on" or "empty") and who's waiting in line.
- Saves from before the canal load fine; ships already partway through it carry on without paying a toll.

- A leg can pass several canals (Duluth to Rotterdam goes through the Soo Locks, the Welland Canal and the St. Lawrence Seaway). Each charges its own toll as the ship enters it, and each adds its XP bonus to the delivery.

## Suez Canal
- A sea-level canal with no locks, in data/canals.json like Panama: its centerline from the Mediterranean off Port Said to Suez Bay (about 96 nm), with the doubled stretches (the Port Said approaches, the Ballah bypass through the New Suez Canal to the Great Bitter Lake, and Suez Bay) and two single-lane stretches (El Qantara, and the Little Bitter Lake to Suez).
- Convoys: ships reaching the canal drop anchor in a cluster at the Port Said anchorage (southbound) or the Suez Bay anchorage (northbound), engines off. Every 90 s a convoy leaves each way at once; a ship arriving within 10 s after its convoy left still joins it. The toll (25% of the leg's base pay) is paid as the convoy leaves; a ship without the money stays at anchor for the next one ("waiting for toll money", red dot on the Ships tab). Recovery boats join convoys but pay nothing.
- Convoy ships enter one after another, fastest first, and sail at half their own speed. In the single-lane stretches they keep 1.6 nm behind the ship ahead (no overtaking); in the doubled stretches they can pass. A ship won't enter a single-lane stretch while a ship the other way is in it, and waits in the doubled stretch before it. The two convoys normally meet in the long doubled middle. A ship held up behind a slower one burns fuel and wears only for the distance it makes, so the fuel check before leaving port (which counts the half-speed canal) always holds.
- Supertankers are too deep for Suez fully loaded: they unload 25% of their cargo to pass (like the real SUMED pipeline), so the leg pays 25% less, and the toll is on the reduced pay.
- Deliveries through Suez get +50% XP. No breakdowns between the anchorages and the exit.
- On the map: the canal is drawn as one channel in single-lane stretches and two side by side in doubled ones (ships keep right); ships at anchor sit in rings round the anchorage. Zoomed in: "Port Said anchorage · next convoy 45 s" and "Suez Bay anchorage · ...", plus Great Bitter Lake, New Suez Canal and Ismailia. Clicking the canal or an anchorage opens a popup: time to the next convoys, and each way the ships at anchor, those waiting for toll money, those entering, and the convoy under way.
- Ship status: "At Port Said anchorage, next southbound convoy in 45 s", "Joining the southbound Suez Canal convoy (2nd in line to enter)", "In the southbound Suez Canal convoy (3rd of 7), held behind Big Blue", "Waiting in a passing stretch for the northbound convoy to clear".
- The route screen shows each Suez leg's toll, +50% XP, "up to 1 min 30 s waiting for a convoy", and a Supertanker's "unloads 25% to pass".

## Great Lakes
- The five Great Lakes (and Lake St. Clair) are water, reached up the St. Lawrence from its estuary, with the Welland Canal (Ontario to Erie), the Detroit and St. Clair rivers (Erie to Huron), the Straits of Mackinac (Huron to Michigan) and the St. Marys River (Huron to Superior).
- Only ships that fit the St. Lawrence Seaway (about 226 m long) can sail to a Great Lakes port: the Scooter 20, Feeder 100, Feedermax 200, Laker, Grain Laker, Cattle Coaster, Livestock Carrier, Coastal and Car Hopper, and both recovery boats (the Buffalo and the Mammoth) so lake ships can be rescued. Bigger ships can't be given routes there or be bought at a lake hub. Recovery boats of either model can be based at a lake hub and recover ships lost on lake routes (a Buffalo only the models it carries).
- Three lock systems (type locks in data/canals.json), 3 s per lock step as at Panama:
  - St. Lawrence Seaway (Montreal to Lake Ontario): 7 single-chamber locks (St. Lambert, Côte-Sainte-Catherine, Lower and Upper Beauharnois, Snell, Eisenhower, Iroquois). Toll 10% of the leg's base pay, +25% XP.
  - Welland Canal (Lake Ontario to Lake Erie): single-chamber Locks 1–3, 7 and 8, and the twinned Flight Locks 4–6 (a lane each way). Toll 10%, +25% XP.
  - Soo Locks (Lake Huron to Lake Superior): one step with 3 parallel chambers, each taking a ship either way. No toll, +25% XP.
- Single (shared) chambers take one ship at a time either way. A chamber is left at the level for the other way, so a ship the same way waits 3 s while it's turned around; with ships waiting both ways, a lock alternates direction. Otherwise the Panama rules apply: lines, engines off, no breakdowns in the lock systems, toll on entering (or wait at the entrance).
- Legs between two ports on the upper lakes (Superior, Michigan, Huron) pay +20% (the ore and grain trade).

- Container prices differ around the lakes (markets.json island_markets "Great Lakes", applied after the smoothing that otherwise prices the lakes alike), so a Scooter 20 can pay its way from the big lake ports: Chicago (rail-hub imports: cheap toys and clothing, dear furniture and coffee) against Milwaukee (the reverse), about $1,700-2,000 a trip each way; and Lake Erie (Cleveland, Detroit, Toledo: toys and furniture a little cheaper, clothing and coffee a little dearer) against Lake Ontario (Toronto, Hamilton: toys and furniture 17% dearer, clothing and coffee 11% cheaper), about $2,200-2,800 Cleveland to Toronto and $3,700-4,100 back through the Welland Canal. Lake Ontario has the biggest differences because every way into it pays a 10% toll (Welland or St. Lawrence), so no outside port can cash in on them.
## Rough seas
- South of 60°S, ships wear twice as fast. The fuel check before leaving port and route ranges allow for it, so some long Antarctic legs within a ship's nominal range are refused ("too far for one tank").
- Legs to or from a port south of 60°S (the Antarctic stations) pay +50%, and so earn +50% XP for the ship, the company and the hub where they're unloaded.
- The route screen marks those legs "rough seas: +50% pay and XP, 2x wear".

## Sea animals
- Just for looking at (they never affect ships): 28 pods of 8 species to start with (see below; 112 pods of 54 species now, with the fish, sharks, seals, sea lions and otters) (data/sea_life.json; scripts/sim/sea_life.gd): bottlenose and spinner dolphins, orcas, and humpback, blue, gray, sperm and southern right whales, 2 to 5 pods each, each pod its real size (bottlenose 8-15, spinners 80-200, orcas 4-15, sperm whale families 10-14 and a bachelor group of 3, the great whales 1-4).

A pod of fish is a school, of sharks sharks, of seals or sea lions a colony and of otters a raft in their popups; the field guide's Sea animals tab groups them (whales, dolphins and orcas; seals, sea lions and otters; sharks; game fish). 112 pods of 54 species in all.

## Wildlife
- Every land animal and every bird is an individual that lives on the map for good (scripts/sim/wildlife.gd Wildlife; data/wildlife.json, with the species' names, looks and facts in data/birds.json), just for looking at, except that the birds following a ship boost it (see Bird boosts under Birds). Worked out from the data and the clocks alone, so every player sees much the same and nothing is saved. Built off the main thread a few seconds into a session (homes keep to the land, so it waits for the map's land); till then there's none. About 2,500 land animals in 456 groups and some 16,500 birds in 2,500 colonies. A group's home away from its species' spot is only taken where all the ground it roams (its roam_km and its members' spread, round its home and halfway out) is land (Wildlife._range_on_land()), so no herd wanders into the sea (southern India's elephants did).
- Each is drawn from above at its size in the world (exaggerated so it reads, like the ships: an elephant 13.5 km, a penguin 4.5 km), growing and shrinking with the map, so they show once you're zoomed in (from about 3 px), on the full-color map only, not in locked regions; turned the way it's heading, stepping as it walks (a second drawing with its legs swapped, or a bird's wings), dimmed asleep (art/wildlife: each land animal and its step, imported at 4x so they stay sharp close in).
- Land animals live in groups (a herd, a pride, a mother and cubs: their sizes from birds.json land_animals group and young), twice wildlife.json groups round each of the map's spots for the species (the first at the spot, the rest within spread_km of it, on land), each with its home if it has one, drawn on the map: penguins' rookeries, polar bears' snow dens, brown bears' and tigers' caves, pandas' dens, kiwis' burrows, koalas' and lemurs' trees, toucans' and quetzals' nest holes, gorillas' and orangutans' leafy nests, elephants' waterholes, bison's and water buffalo's wallows and lions' rocks; camels, giraffes, zebras, kangaroos, moose, reindeer and llamas just roam and bed down where they are. When its species is up (by day; lions, tigers, koalas and kiwis by night; kangaroos and moose at dusk and dawn, by the sun there) a group roams its range (roam_km) round its home from spot to spot at its walking pace (kmh), grazing and resting between, the young close by their mother; the rest of the time it's at its home (or bedded down where it last was), going and coming back as the day turns. Brown bears sleep the winter (December to March) in their caves, and polar bear mothers with cubs keep to their snow dens December to February: the den shows its z's. Reindeer go 4 degrees south for November to April and zebras 1.9 degrees north (the Serengeti to the Mara) for July to October, the herd walking there over 10 days.
- Birds live in colonies at the ports: each port has its gulls (8, 12, 16 or 20 by its size band, 4 more at a hub, shared between up to three of the species whose ranges cover it, or the nearest), and up to 2 species of raptors (and an owl), 2 of cormorants, 3 of waders and 1 of sea ducks whose ranges cover it (each living there or not by birds.json's chance for it, in its numbers: up to 2 raptors, 8 cormorants, 3 waders and a raft of 16; the herons', egrets' and bitterns' chances were cut by 30% on 2026-10-10). Each colony's home is on the shore either side of its port (the same spots the waders use): a gull or cormorant colony at the water's edge, an eyrie, heronry, nest in the reeds or nest on the ground inland. A colony whose species' range only covers its port some months spends the rest at the nearest port its range covers then (or in its range there), flying between the two in a V (all its birds, along the shortest way round the globe) over the first 10 days of each stay; at the port it's visiting it's one of that port's birds. Seabirds nest in colonies at some of the ports in their range (30% of them; their numbers three times their follow count) and forage out to sea round them, gliding from spot to spot within 60% of their range (albatrosses 2,500 km, shearwaters 1,800, terns 350, pelicans 150, condors 300), and follow ships that pass. Each bird lives as a permanent map object: see Birds.
- Movement goes by the life clock: calendar time at the game's speed, but never slower than 1x, so the wildlife carries on while the game's paused; when things happen (day and night, the seasons) goes by the calendar.
- Click an animal for its group's popup (LandLifePopup, following it): the species with its picture, its group ("A pride of 6 with a cub", "A mother with 2 cubs", "On its own"), what they're doing now ("On the move", "3 grazing, 2 resting", "Asleep at the cave", "Hibernating in the den"), its home and when it's there ("A cave, back by night; they sleep the winter away in it"), where it lives, its habits and a fact. Click a home for its popup (HomePopup): "Rookery (Penguins)", "Colony (Herring Gull)": who lives there and what they're doing now (home for the night, most out and about by day, away at Lisbon for now, on the wing to Lisbon), for a bird colony when it's there through the year ("Here April to September; the rest of the year at Lisbon", or all year) and any eggs or chicks in the season (its hemisphere's spring and early summer), and a fact. A V on the wing or a seabird out at sea opens the birds popup ("Arctic Tern on the wing: 12 flying to Cape Town"). Each counts as seen for the field guide.

## Birds
- Just for looking at (they never affect ships). Every bird is a permanent map object (scripts/sim/bird_life.gd BirdLife): one of the wildlife's colonies' birds (see Wildlife), always somewhere, doing something, never appearing or vanishing. Its day goes in spells (each class its own length: gulls 18-40 s, raptors 30-70, cormorants 25-55, waders 40-90, sea ducks 45-100, seabirds 50-130 s, each bird its own phase); at the start of each it picks what to do next by its class and the hour, then flies there from wherever it was (its last spell's end, worked out again if no one was looking), smoothly and along a gentle curve, at its class's pace (faster if it must, to arrive within 80% of the spell), and does it. Its position is worked out from the bird clock alone (real seconds, so they carry on while the game's paused, at their own pace at any game speed), so it's the same whoever looks, and nothing is saved. By night (before 5:30 and after 20:00 by the sun; night herons the other way round) birds sleep at their colony's home; sea ducks and seabirds out at sea sleep on the water. data/birds.json has the species, and scripts/sim/birds.gd their ranges and seasons.
- Gulls: 15 species, each in its real range (Herring, Great Black-backed, Lesser Black-backed, Yellow-legged, Black-headed, Ring-billed, Laughing, California, Western, Glaucous-winged, Black-tailed, Heermann's, Sooty, Kelp and Silver gulls), some moving between summer and winter ranges. Every port has its gull colony (see Wildlife). By day each gull circles the port, perches on the port's rocks (each bird its own spot, the rocks' filled first, then the breakwater's arms), swims off it (every bird on the water sits bobbing and now and then paddles about along a slow wandering line: gulls, seabirds, cormorants and sea ducks alike; BirdLife._paddle()), follows a ship within 60 km of the port (flying behind and off to one side of it, wherever it goes, till the spell ends), or rests at the colony; by night most sleep at the colony and a few sit on the sea.
- Seabirds: 16 species (Andean and California condors; Wandering, Black-browed and Laysan albatrosses; Arctic, Common, Caspian and Royal terns; Brown, Great White and Australian pelicans; Sooty and Manx shearwaters; Magnificent and Great frigatebirds, with their long angular wings, forked tails and scarlet throats), each with seasonal ranges they migrate between (Arctic Terns from pole to pole, Sooty Shearwaters round the Pacific). Each wanders out over the sea from its colony a stretch at a time (within 60% of its range, then up to 120 km about that), gliding in wide circles and sitting on the sea (paddling about now and then; a frigatebird never settles on the water and soars on through the night instead), and takes up following a ship that passes within 250 km (steadily by species and ship), till its spell ends; by night it sleeps on the water where it is.
- Raptors (data/birds.json raptors): 15 eagles, hawks and the osprey, drawn with broad, finger-tipped wings (an osprey's bent at the wrist), a fanned tail and a hooked bill: Bald, White-tailed, Steller's Sea, African Fish, White-bellied Sea, Golden and Wedge-tailed eagles; the Osprey; Red-tailed, Rough-legged, Swainson's, Harris's and Cooper's hawks, the Northern Goshawk and the Common Buzzard; each with its seasonal ranges (Swainson's hawks migrate from the Great Plains through Central America and Panama to Argentina; Steller's sea eagles winter on Hokkaido's drift ice; Rough-legged hawks breed on the Arctic tundra; Peregrine falcons on sea cliffs the world over, Gyrfalcons round the Arctic and Brown falcons across Australia, with swept pointed wings and long narrow tails). They never follow ships. By day each perches on the land behind its port, soars high in slow circles over the land, or hunts out over the sea (circling, now and then plunging in with a splash and climbing again); by night it sleeps at its eyrie. A bird's shadow falls further off the higher it flies.
- Owls (raptors of kind Owl): 8 of them, drawn with a big round head (a pale facial disc, big eyes, ear tufts on the horned owls), broad rounded wings and a short round tail: Snowy (the Arctic, south in winter to North America's coasts, Scandinavia, Russia and Hokkaido), Barn, Short-eared, Great Horned, Eurasian Eagle-, Blakiston's Fish, Pel's Fishing and Powerful owls. They go about it as the other raptors do, but by night, and sleep on their perches by day (the snowy owl hunts by day, as it really does: birds.json active "day"). A port has up to one owl species besides its 2 other raptors (Wildlife OWLS_A_PORT). They have no calls yet.
- Cormorants (data/birds.json cormorants): 9 species (Great, Double-crested, Pelagic, Neotropic, Cape, Japanese, Little Pied and Guanay cormorants, and the European Shag), each where it really lives. Drawn with a long neck stretched out ahead, a small head (a pale cheek or bright throat on some) and a thin bill. A colony keeps together: perched on the rocks or the breakwater (now and then holding their wings out to dry), swimming low off it and diving for fish (a dark shape under a ripple while under), or circling wide round the port together; by night they sleep at their colony.
- Herons, egrets, bitterns and cranes (data/birds.json waders): 22 species (Grey, Great Blue, Purple, Goliath, Striated and Black-crowned Night herons; Great, Little, Snowy, Cattle and Reddish egrets; Eurasian, American, Least and Australasian bitterns; Common, Sandhill, Red-crowned, Whooping, Grey Crowned, Sarus cranes and the Brolga), each where and when it really is (Whooping Cranes winter only at Aransas on the Texas coast; Red-crowned Cranes on Hokkaido; Common and Sandhill cranes migrate). They live along their port's shore either side of its marina: the shore is found once per port from the land as drawn (the water's edge, and a spot inland), never on a marina's quay. Drawn standing from above with a long shadow cast to the side (legs, neck and head: they're tall), the body with folded wings, neck and dagger bill; in flight with broad fingered wings and legs trailing (a crane's neck stretched out, a heron's drawn back). By day each stands at the water's edge, neck poised, or stalks slowly along it, flying between spots along the shore; a bittern always stands hidden in a clump of reeds swaying in the wind, bill to the sky; by night they sleep hunched at the heronry.
- Birds that fly in Vs in life do so on their migrations (the leader at the point, the rest back along either arm by turns, 4.5 km apart): each bird flies from wherever it was into its place over the first 25 s.
- Sea ducks (data/birds.json sea_ducks): 9 eiders and scoters (Common, King, Spectacled and Steller's eiders; Common, Black, Surf, White-winged and Velvet scoters), each with its real migration: breeding on Arctic and northern coasts and lakes in summer and wintering on northern coasts (the whole world's Spectacled Eiders in a few holes in the Bering Sea ice; Steller's Eiders in Norway's Varangerfjord; scoters off Britain, both North American coasts, and Japan). A raft lives off its port day and night, the ducks together on one of the sea spots off it, drawn swimming from above, drakes in their pattern (eiders white-backed with black sides and green napes, King Eiders with blue-grey heads and orange bills, scoters black with bright bills, white face and wing patches) and hens brown, drifting and turning a little; by day each dives in its own time; now and then the whole raft flies round the port together and settles somewhere new.
- Bird seasons: the data's months read in as floats, so a season's months are matched as numbers either way.
- On the World map (MapView _draw_birds()), each bird is drawn at its size in the world with the ships: a dot of its back's color while it's under 4 px across (from 1.6 px), then its drawing from above in its species' colors (mantle, head, wingtips, bill), flapping, gliding or folded, with its shadow on the water further off the higher it is, turned the way it's heading. Clicking one opens its colony's popup (remembering the bird clicked): the species with its picture, name, Latin name, how many and what they're doing now ("5 circling the port, 4 perched, 3 resting at home"), or where they're flying to on a migration. A port's or ship's birds list the same way. Each species has a Follow button at its right: the map follows one of its birds (the one clicked, if the popup was opened by clicking a bird; else one out and about if any is, one following the ship for a ship's birds), keeping it in the middle wherever it goes, zoomed in 3 times as close as when following a ship; the button reads Following while it does (pressed again, or a click or a drag on the map, or following a ship, lets it go; MapView.follow_bird()). Port popups say "Gulls: Herring Gull (16), Yellow-legged Gull (8)" (each species once, all its flocks there added up, the most first); ship popups say "Following: Herring Gull (3), Wandering Albatross (2)" while any are following it.
- Worked out and drawn in C++ (native/src/bird_sim.cpp BirdSim, a GDExtension: see native/README.md) when the native library is there, the GDScript doing the same where it isn't. Drawn cheaply so busy harbours stay smooth: only birds whose whole spell keeps them near the view are worked out at all (BirdLife.bird_bounds(), till the spell ends or its own colony sets off or arrives (a colony's change reworks only its own birds' kept states and bounds, and the still layer is drawn again only if any of them were on it, so the turn of a month, when hundreds of colonies set off, costs no more than those in view) or arrives), each bird's drawing is one mesh made once per species, wing spread and size (MapView.ShapeRecorder), and birds keeping still for 4 s or more (standing, perched, resting or asleep) are drawn on their own layer, redrawn only when the view changes or just before the first of them moves.
- Field guide: the square paw-print button at the map's top right opens a guide to every bird (gulls, seabirds, raptors, cormorants, herons, egrets, bitterns and cranes, and sea ducks), sea animal (with the waters its pods visit) and land animal on the map (with its group size and habits), a tab each, each with its picture, where to see it and a fact. Esc, X or a click outside closes it.
- Bird boosts (scripts/sim/bird_boosts.gd, BirdBoosts): every species that follows ships gives the ship a little of its own, four shares: speed gained, wear saved, fuel saved, and storm shelter (the storm's slowing and wearing shrugged off in part). Gulls are small change (1-2% of one or two), terns are quick (+2-3% speed, a little fuel), pelicans save fuel and wear (2-4% each), condors keep a ship sound (3-4% less wear), frigatebirds and shearwaters shelter it (15-20%), and the albatrosses are the prize (the Wandering Albatross +4% speed, 2% fuel, 25% shelter). A species counts once it has a bird aboard, 15% more for each bird up to three more; all the species following add up, capped at +12% speed, 25% wear, 15% fuel and 60% shelter. Looked at again every 5 s of play (GameState.bird_boosts()); applied in the storm factors (speed, wear, shelter) and the ship's fuel burn; the ship's popup says what its followers give it. In a Divine Actions game the divine blessing comes on top.

## Weather
- Storms (scripts/sim/weather.gd, game_config weather), in saves founded with Moderate or Harsh storms. They form in their real basins and seasons by the calendar, each with a name used in turn: Atlantic and East Pacific hurricanes (Jun-Nov, peak Aug-Oct), West Pacific typhoons (all year, peak Jul-Oct), North Indian cyclones (Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea, around the monsoon), South Indian, Australian and South Pacific cyclones (Nov-Apr), and winter gales in the North Atlantic and North Pacific (Oct-Mar) and the Southern Ocean (all year, worst in the austral winter). About 120 a year at Moderate (Harsh: half as many again), up to 8 or so at once.
- Tropical storms drift west and poleward at about 10-12 knots, then curve away east (and speed up) past their recurve latitude; gales run east at about 25 knots. Each builds up over the first half of its life (3-12 calendar days, a day being 72 s of play) and fades over the rest; its strength at a point is that times how near its core it is (full within 35% of its radius, about 200-500 nm, fading to nothing at the edge).
- From company level 6 (with breakdowns), a ship in a storm sails slower (by up to 40% at the core at Moderate, 60% at Harsh) and wears faster (up to 2x / 3x), and its breakdown chance rises the same way. Fuel per mile stays the same (the engines just make less way), so a storm alone never strands a ship that set off with enough fuel. A hurricane, typhoon or cyclone strong enough over a port (85% of full strength at Moderate, 70% at Harsh) closes it: ships there wait in port, and ships bound for it wait 25 nm off it with engines off, until it passes ("Waiting off Charleston for Hurricane Ana to pass"). These waits aren't holds: no red dot, nothing for the player to do, and no random breakdowns while waiting (nor in an icebreaker's convoy). Recovery boats go in regardless.
- Warnings in the activity log, for the HQ, hubs and every port a ship is at, bound for or has on its route: a tropical storm heading for one (about 2 calendar days ahead), one closing and reopening, and each ship sailing into a storm.
- Wind (scripts/sim/wind.gd): everywhere, all the time, following the real prevailing winds by latitude: the north-east (south-east) trade winds in the tropics, the westerlies of the middle latitudes (strongest in the Southern Ocean's roaring forties), the polar easterlies, and near calm in the doldrums and horse latitudes, wandering in direction (up to about 45 degrees) and strength with slow gusty variation over thousands of miles and minutes of play, and wound round every storm (anticlockwise in the north, clockwise in the south), strongest at its core. The same for every player. On the World map it shows as gusts: short pale streaks blowing its way, drifting and fading, none in a calm, more, longer and brighter the stronger it blows. Ships at anchor face into it.
- Clouds (the map's sky, scripts/ui/globe.gdshader): drifting cloud over the globe, thicker along the equator and the middle latitudes' storm tracks and thinner over the subtropics, clearing as the map zooms in (from 7 to 24 px a degree) so ships show.
- Night and day (scripts/ui/night.gdshader; MapView _update_night(), _draw_lights(); World map only): the side of the globe turned from the sun is dark, with dusk and dawn a soft band between (a terminator, so one side of the world is in its night while the other has its day). The sun stands over the sub-solar point of the calendar's moment (game_config calendar: 20 calendar minutes a second of play, so a day is 72 s at 1x), tilted with the seasons (23.44 degrees); the calendar runs on Greenwich time (GameState.clock_longitude() is 0), so the sun is over Greenwich at 12:00 GMT, the rest of the world an hour on for every 15 degrees east (the birds' nights and the deck crews' watches go by each place's own hour); the top bar shows London time, GMT in winter and BST (an hour ahead) from the last Sunday in March to the last Sunday in October, marked GMT or BST. Two darkenings: 90% of the map art, land drawings, marinas and weather is lost at the darkest, and 60% more of the lanes, ports, labels, birds and ships over them (so the names still read), and over it all the lights: ships' navigation lights (red to port, green to starboard, white at the masthead and stern, each with a halo) and the warm glow of their decks with a few deck lamps, each marina's entrance lights (red and green, haloed), lamps along its quay (a warm glow over the HQ and the industry), a lamp every 5 dock units down each pier (white at its tip), along the basin's walls and round the jetty's arms, its HQ lit (a glow in its company's colour and warm light, its windows lit, more and cooler the bigger it has grown, a few dark; red aircraft lights on its corners from level 6) and each industry building under an orange sodium floodlight with a lamp at each corner of its yard and a few lit windows (all drawn once into the marina's lights item, again when the HQ or industry changes), its town's windows lit over a glow across the town, and its lighthouse sweeping two opposite beams round once every 6 s of play, and, zoomed out past the marinas, the ports' towns as warm glows (7 to 18 px by size band). The ships' and the marinas' lights (lighthouse, entrance, quay) come on under a storm's cloud by day as well, as dark as the cloud is thick (MapView _dark_at(): full under the core, thinning to the storm's edge); the towns' glow is night's only. Birds already sleep by their port's night. Nothing else changes with the dark.
- Ships at anchor in a storm (waiting for a berth, idle, tugs, recovery boats and other service ships) swing and pitch about their anchors, more the stronger the storm there; one over 50% of its strength at the port batters each ship there once, taking 20% of its maintenance ("Sea Otter was battered at anchor off Rome by Storm Ciaran: maintenance down to 64%").

## Seasonal closures
- In saves founded with Seasonal closures (scripts/sim/seasons.gd, game_config seasons), from company level 6 (with breakdowns and storms, so a new company, which always starts on 1 January, isn't iced in from its first minute). Real dates, every year:
  - St. Lawrence Seaway and Welland Canal locks: shut 31 Dec to 22 Mar. Soo Locks: 15 Jan to 25 Mar.
  - The Great Lakes ports: iced in 15 Jan to 20 Feb, but not in the game's first year (2000). The ice (on the map, and the icebreakers' area) covers the lakes themselves, a circle each (Superior, Michigan, Huron, Georgian Bay, Erie, Ontario), so it never reaches the Atlantic coast.
  - The Antarctic stations: shut by sea ice 1 Apr to 31 Oct (reachable only in the austral summer).
  - Nain (Labrador): 1 Dec to 30 Jun. Magadan and Vanino (Sea of Okhotsk): 1 Jan to 15 Apr.
  - Gulf of Finland ice (within 180 nm of the gulf: St Petersburg, Ust-Luga, Helsinki, Tallinn): 10 Jan to 31 Mar. It stays open, but ships crawl through at 45% speed and 1.5x wear (fuel per mile the same).
- A ship won't start a leg it can't finish before a closure: its destination shut when it would arrive, or a canal on the way shut while it's sailing (checked about daily). It waits in port ("Waiting for the Welland Canal to reopen on 22 Mar (its winter shutdown)"), not a hold: no red dot. Ships already at sea finish their leg; ships can always leave a port that's closing.
- Warnings in the activity log for closures touching the player's ports (HQ, hubs, where ships are or are bound, their routes) or canals their ships use: 10 calendar days ahead ("The Welland Canal closes for its winter shutdown on 31 Dec: ships won't start legs they can't finish before then."), then when it shuts and reopens, and when the Gulf of Finland ice sets in and clears.
- On the World map, while a closure is in force: closed ports have an icy ring, shut canals are frosted over along their path, and the frozen sea is a solid white ice sheet with a soft edge and faint cracks. That covers each closure's ice (the Great Lakes, Hudson Bay, the Labrador and Beaufort seas, the Okhotsk and White seas, the Arctic routes, the Antarctic coast) and every ice zone in season, such as the Baltic's. The sheet lies on sea and lakes, never over land. An icebreaker under way breaks a dark open-water channel through it, edged with broken ice, which freezes over again in about a minute of play. The Northern Sea Route and the Northwest Passage are labeled on the map from mid zoom (game_config label). A port's popup lists its closure ("Closes for ice from 15 Jan to 25 Mar", or "Closed by ice until 25 Mar"), and the route screen notes each leg's closures. The Tutorial's Seasonal closures tip explains them once they apply.
- Icebreakers (see Icebreakers) lead ships through the Great Lakes and Sea of Okhotsk ice and keep the Gulf of Finland's fairways open.

While the ice is in, an icebreaker stays wherever in its area it docked, ready for the next convoy; otherwise it goes home to its station port. First come, first served.

Landed catch earns XP like a delivery over the miles out and back, and the seafood families have achievement stars in crates.
- The field guide has a Catch tab: every crab (art/catch/<id>.svg, drawn from above in its real colours and build) and fish, family by family, with where and when it's caught, how, what a crate fetches and a fact. AI rivals don't fish (they may run seafood carriers). Deckhand Joe introduces fishing in the tutorial.

## People on deck
- Zoomed right in (the map now zooms to 6,000 px a degree, from 800; people show from about 1,600), every ship has its people about its deck, seen from above: a hat (or hair) on a head, shoulders in their shirt's colour, legs in their trousers' colour showing as they walk, and a shadow (art/people/deck_people.svg, one atlas; MapView _draw_deck_crew(), one batch of quads a ship; scripts/ui/deck_crew.gd DeckCrew works out where everyone is from the ship, its own clock and the time, nothing saved). Where they can go is each ship art's layout (data/deck_layouts.json: the open deck they wander, the side gangways beside the cargo, the house's door, the bridge wings, the bow and stern stations and the fishing gear's places).
- Who's out: a big crew keeps three 8-hour watches, a third of the crew (captains and crew from GameState.crew_of(), at most 60) on deck at once, changing at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 by the ship's own clock (its longitude), the outgoing watch walking back in to the door as the next comes out. A crew of 8 or fewer works 14-hour days and turns in in turn round the clock, so the boat never stops and someone's always at the helm. The officer of the watch keeps to the bridge wings (the helm on a small boat); the rest wander the decks, stand looking over the side, and go to work: two at the bow and two at the stern with lines while the ship comes alongside, lies there its first and last moments and pulls out; an anchor watch at the windlass at the anchorage; and at the fishing grounds the gear: by the rods while trolling, a strike every 40 seconds or so (one fighting the fish with the rod bent and the line taut astern, one beside them with the gaff), at the net reel, at the trawl winches, or at a crabber's pot hauler with the rest carrying pots from it to the stacks and back.
- Dock workers (the port's, in hard hats and hi-vis) stand at the bollards on the quay beside a ship coming alongside or pulling out, and through its stop a gang works its cargo along the quay beside it, as many as it needs (a container ship 2 more a tier, bulk carriers and livestock 1 more a tier, a tanker 2 at its manifold, car carriers 3 and up, reefers 3 and up, a fishing boat 1). ## Ocean currents
- 28 of the world's major surface currents (data/currents.json, scripts/sim/currents.gd Currents): the Florida Current, Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift; the Canary, North and South Equatorial, Caribbean, North Brazil, Brazil and Benguela currents; the Agulhas, the Indian Ocean's South Equatorial, the Somali and Monsoon currents (which run the other way in the northern winter, with the monsoon), the Leeuwin and East Australian currents; the Kuroshio, North Pacific, California, Alaska, Oyashio, the Pacific's equatorial currents and its Equatorial Counter Current, the Humboldt; the Antarctic Circumpolar, East Greenland and Labrador currents. Each is a path the water flows along with its core speed (knots), how far out it's felt (fading to its edge) and its strength month by month (1 its usual; below 0 the other way), laid once onto a half-degree grid (the strongest current a cell). The month is the calendar's (GameData.month, set by GameState as it turns), whatever the seasonal closures setting.
- Ships feel them: a lane's speed zones (GameData.lane_zones()) are split where the current's push along the lane changes, each sped up or slowed by (15 kn + the current along the way) / 15 kn, at most 35% either way (game_config currents). Fuel burns by the second, so a ship with the current also burns less fuel a leg, and one against it more; the fuel a ship needs for a leg (Ship.fuel_needed()) counts the currents of the month it sets off in, so it never runs dry for them.
- Second ways round: for each lane, each way, the lane tool (tools/build_map_data.gd --currents-only, after the lanes and channels) looks for a current running alongside it (within 360 nm, the same way, at least 300 nm of it) and plans a way that leaves the lane at least 150 nm from the port it sails from, rides the current and rejoins the lane at least 150 nm from the port it's bound for, routed through the sea grid like any lane. It's kept if it saves at least 2.5% of the leg's time in some months and is no more than 1.2 times as long as the stretch it leaves out; the months it's quicker in go with it (SeaLaneData variant_*). A ship setting off on a leg takes the second way round in those months (GameData.way_now()), and keeps to the way it chose (and the currents of the month it chose it in) to the end of the leg, saved with it (Ship leg_key, leg_current, leg_month), the host's way sent to the other players. So ships from the US to Europe ride the Gulf Stream, and with the Somali Current turning with the monsoon, the way along the Horn of Africa changes over the year. Lanes through a canal, and lanes to or from the Arctic, have no second way. Its status says "Sailing to Rotterdam on the Gulf Stream".
- On the map the currents flow: dashes drifting along three lines across each current's width the way the water runs (back the other way in the months it turns), warm currents red-orange and cold ones blue, brighter the stronger it runs this month; only the middle line far out; fading in from a mid zoom and out again close in (MapView _draw_currents(); stretches off screen skipped, about 0.7 ms a frame).

## Long-term moorings
- Every port's town is laid on a street grid; where the coast runs through that grid (a port on a headland, a spit, an island or a river mouth) the streets would have sat on the water. Those runs of street are the port's long-term moorings instead (MapView _lay_moorings(), laid with the town once per port): each becomes a pontoon, a floating walkway along the street's line with dark floats under it and plank seams close in, with finger piers out from both sides and a slip between each finger and the next, every finger and slip only where the water really is and clear of the marina's own ground (the quay, the basin, the harbour inside the jetty), as long as the longest ship of any kind (the Mammoth) and its beam and a gap wide (since 2026-10-13: the moorings are every ship's long-term parking, the recovery boats' too; before, the port's own longest cargo ship), for a ship to come straight in, bow to the pontoon; a cleat at each finger's root, a lamp at each pontoon's end at night. The quay road and the cross roads are laid first, then the avenue and the streets, each keeping clear of the others' fingers; and the side streets' lines along either side of the harbour just outside the quay land (one row at a Small or Medium port, two at a Large, three at a Giant or a hub), so a port with water beside its marina (Shanghai, Singapore, Hong Kong, Auckland, Honolulu, Los Angeles...) gets a mooring field flanking it. A pontoon must lie along a shore or the quay, or touch land at an end (none floats off by itself), and is worth building only for 2 slips or more. The roads keep to their runs on land. Wood at ports of 8 berths or fewer, concrete at the bigger. The side rows start beyond the way ships round the jetty's end (1.5 jetty radii out), and a row only beyond a built row nearer the quay, so no field floats off by itself; a street pontoon stays within 14 units of its own quay (not out by some other islet). With the game's 322 ports, 146 have moorings, about 850 slips in all.
- A mooring field behind a spit or a headland (Surabaya's, across its peninsula) is sailed to by water: where a leg of the way out round the jetty would cross land, the way goes round through the water instead, found on a grid of the water round the port (made once per port on a worker thread; till it's ready, a ship lies at the anchorage), cut down to the corners it needs; the staging point outside a slip is pulled back along the slip to the last of the water. A mooring with no way to it by water at all (a field on a lake behind the town) is drawn but never given a ship.
- A cargo ship lying in port without a berth (waiting for a load, idle, paused, or for fuel money; service ships too, if they fit) takes the first free slip it fits, or if the port has none (or they're full) its spot at the anchorage as before; it sails to it from the entrance out round the jetty's end on its side, round the back of the quay if the slip is behind it, up to the slip's mouth and straight in, and leaves the same way: backing straight out of the slip to its mouth, turning there on its thrusters, and back round by the way it came, at its own speed (its time in to a berth counts all of it, as does its time out to the moorings, so it never hurries). An idle or paused ship stays at the moorings, its long-term parking, till it has somewhere to go: it isn't brought in to a pier while the port has room (only running ships are), and one done with its stop pulls out to the moorings. Tugs still moor along the jetty's arms.

## Tugs
- The Tug (ship_models.json tug; level 3, $50,000, 4 nm/s): a small black tug, drawn at sea with a fender all round, a white wheelhouse and a funnel. Docked, a tug moors along the inside of its marina's jetty, not at the anchorage: each in its own slot (the first free one, kept till it leaves), the two arms by turns, from a little over halfway round each arm (54 degrees from the entrance) towards the entrance, 0.6 dock units apart, bow towards it (one each arm at a Small or Medium port, two at a Large or bigger; map_view _jetty_pose()); with those all taken, on round towards the arms' land ends. It comes home from just inside the entrance across the outer harbour to the land end of its own arm and along the arm to its slot (_tug_home_way()), and leaves along the arm to just inside the entrance and out, so it never crosses the jetty, the basin's corners or the pier tips (on the gray route map, a small black tick across the port's outline, reaching a little inside and out of it, side by side 3 px apart at the top). A service ship like recovery boats: no route or cargo, pays its own fuel and repairs, never lost, no breakdowns.
- Slots come from the routes, not levels: two for each port on any cargo ship's route (game_config tugs per_port), e.g. one ship on Rotterdam-Hamburg allows 4. The Shop card says "Owned 1 / 4 (2 per route port)", and when they're full the button says "Route to more ports for more". With no routes yet, buying explains that tugs are based at the ports on your routes.
- Each new tug launches straight at the route port with the fewest tugs (ties: the port on the most ships' routes), so they spread evenly; the buy popup shows where (the port can't be picked). When the routes change, a tug at a port no longer on any route (or over its port's two) is re-based to the nearest route port still short of tugs that a full tank (1,500 nm) can reach, and sails there once free; with nowhere to go it stays put.
- Job: a ship lost within a tug's reach (150 nm along its lane, +15% per Range upgrade) of either end of its leg is fetched by the free tugs (docked at their base, with the fuel) based at the nearer end, up to two, before any recovery boat is sent (auto-recovery and the Send Recovery button alike; the button shows "Tugger and Puller → Rotterdam, about 43 s"). They sail out along the lane one after the other (the second leaves 1.5 s after the first, "Following Tugger out to tow Hauler", so they head out in line), stop short ahead of the ship's bow, turn round on a turning circle to face home (3 s), and once both have, tow it in. Both pull from ahead of its bow on tow lines (1.2 tug lengths): one tug on a single line, two side by side in a tight Y, their lines from the bow the same length. Each tows at half its speed (game_config tugs tow_factor), added up, so two bring a ship in twice as fast as one. The towed ship comes round from the way it lay to the way it's towed over its first 3 nm. At the tugs' port the ship is handed over (paid as usual if that's where it was going, else nothing), and the tugs bring it in:
  - With a berth free (it takes one at once, as it would next step): they pull it straight in through the mouth, through the outer harbour and down the channel to where it comes round for its slot. There they let go, easing to a stop beside it, away from its slot and inside the channel, clear of the pier tips (where the channel is too narrow for the second tug beside the first it stops behind it; "Bringing Hauler alongside at Rotterdam"), and lie there while it goes on in by itself at half speed, round into its slot and the last slide onto its pier; then they go out along the channel's middle and home by their arm's land end to their moorings. Its time coming alongside is all this (GameState asks the map: MapView._tug_work()).
  - With no berth free: they cast off at the harbour mouth and go in to their moorings by their arm's land end; the ship glides on to its spot at the anchorage to wait for a berth, as any ship coming in to wait does.
  - Tugs always go the way they face, as boats do (never sideways): along their lanes and the tow's track (the lane, the curve into the harbour, on in through the mouth), round smooth turns (0.7 of their length), backing straight off at most. Every move is worked out ahead from the ship's own way in, so each tug's track is smooth from heading out to docking again.
- Upgrades: tugs level up from their jobs (recovery_xp per_nm for every mile out and back; the company also earns XP for the towed ship's cargo, like a recovery), and spend points on Speed, Range (reach +15%) and Efficiency (fuel), in place of Durability.
- Statuses: "Standing by at Rotterdam", "Heading out to tow Hauler", "Turning round to take Hauler in tow", "Waiting for Puller to take Hauler in tow", "Towing Hauler to Rotterdam with Puller", "Bringing Hauler alongside at Rotterdam with Puller", and on the towed ship "Being towed to Rotterdam by Tugger and Puller". The activity log says "Auto-recovery: Tugger and Puller sent out from Rotterdam for Hauler (about $339)" and "Tugger and Puller towed Hauler in to Rotterdam". A tow under way is saved with the game.
- Tugs show in the Shop's Tugs section, the Ships tab's "Recovery boats and tugs" filter and Finances' Tugs group; a tile reads "Tug at Rotterdam · Lv 2".
- Tugs cost 20% less for every third of the field guide's animals the company has seen (its Field guide stars: 20%, 40%, 60% with all of them; GameState.tug_discount()), for every tug bought from then on, Buy All Tugs too; the Shop's tug card shows "$40,000 (20% off: animals seen)", its price's tooltip how it works.

## The Arctic
- 16 Arctic ports (ports.json arctic: true; ranks 302-317): Murmansk (ice-free, the Atomflot base), Arkhangelsk (White Sea), Sabetta (Yamal LNG), Dudinka (Norilsk's port, up the Yenisei: carved as a channel), Tiksi and Pevek on the Northern Sea Route; Churchill (Hudson Bay grain), Iqaluit, Resolute (Northwest Passage) and Prudhoe Bay (Alaska's North Slope oil); Longyearbyen (Svalbard coal), Sisimiut and Ilulissat (west Greenland); Tromso, Hammerfest (LNG) and Kirkenes (iron ore) in northern Norway. Murmansk, Arkhangelsk and the Norwegian ports are in the Northern Europe market; the rest in a new Arctic one, where most goods are dear (1.7-1.9x) but each port's own export is cheap (markets.json sources).
- Routing runs to 80.5 N (tools/build_map_data.gd), but only lanes to or from an Arctic port may go north of 66 N; between two other ports the Arctic counts as land, so every lane that existed before is unchanged. So the Arctic routes are sailed through Arctic ports: Rotterdam to Pevek goes along the Northern Sea Route, and a summer Europe-Asia run is e.g. Rotterdam - Pevek - Yokohama.
- Seasonal closures (in saves with them), on real dates: the Northern Sea Route (a stretch of sea from the Kara Sea to the Chukchi Sea: every lane through it) open 1 Jul to 15 Nov; the Northwest Passage (the Canadian archipelago) open 15 Aug to 5 Oct; and the ports Churchill (open 11 Jul to 14 Nov), Iqaluit (1 Jul to 31 Oct), Prudhoe Bay (1 Aug to 30 Sep), Ilulissat (16 May to 14 Jan) and Arkhangelsk (16 May to 30 Nov). Murmansk, northern Norway, Longyearbyen and Sisimiut stay open all year (the Gulf Stream). A ship won't start a leg through a closed route it can't finish in time; the route screen lists each leg's closures, and the map shows a closed route iced over.
- Icebreakers: a nuclear icebreaker stationed on the Northern Sea Route (Murmansk, Sabetta, Tiksi, Pevek) or the Northwest Passage (Resolute, Iqaluit, Prudhoe Bay) keeps it open all winter, leading convoys through the ice like the Sea of Okhotsk ones; only a nuclear one can ("Only a nuclear icebreaker can keep the Northern Sea Route open through the Arctic winter."). A diesel one can keep Arkhangelsk open (the White Sea).

### Top bar (on World, Ships, Finances, Shop screens)
- Left: company level with an XP bar, and money. Middle: the company name (in the company color) above the calendar date and time ("Sat 1 Jan 2000, 14:30"). Right: the game speed buttons.
- Calendar: a new company starts on 1 January 2000 at midnight. The clock runs 20 calendar minutes per second of play at 1x (game_config calendar), matched to real ship speeds (a Scooter's 5.44 nm a second is about 16 knots), so voyages take realistic calendar times: Rome to Tunis about 20 hours, Shanghai to Rotterdam about a month; a calendar day passes in about 72 seconds. It follows the game speed (stops when paused). The route screen shows each leg's calendar time too ("1 min 1 s (about 20 h)"), a ship at sea shows when it arrives ("arrives 3 Jan 09:10"), and activity log lines are stamped with the calendar time.
- Game speed: a button for each speed, Pause, 1x, 2x, 4x and 8x, up to the save's fastest game speed (chosen when founding it; a 2x save shows only Pause, 1x and 2x), with the current one highlighted; the F key steps through them in turn (back to Pause after the fastest), and Space pauses, then carries on at the speed it was paused from (neither while typing in a text box; in multiplayer only the host). Paused freezes the world; faster speeds run it that much faster (ships, breakdowns, XP, finances, prices); each session starts at 1x.
- Esc (once nothing else wants it: an open popup or the Route Assignment screen close first) opens a menu over the dimmed game: Continue, Save ("Saved at ..."), Settings, Exit to Menu and Exit to Desktop (both save first). In single player the game pauses while it's open and carries on at its speed after Continue (or Esc again); in multiplayer it keeps running. A joined player can't save (the host saves every company) and leaving keeps their company going; a host leaving ends the game for everyone, as Leave Game does.
- Settings (Esc menu; scripts/ui/settings_page.gd SettingsPage, scripts/autoload/settings.gd Settings): a page over the dimmed menu in two tabs (Esc, X or a click outside closes it). General: "Ships seen from afar", one of Small dots (default: ships drawn their true size in the world, a 2 px dot once too small to make out), Bigger dots (5 px) or Ships grow as you zoom out (never smaller than 0.7/SHIP_SIZE px a dock unit, the size they kept zoomed out before ships were sized in the world, so their art shows; easing back to their true size from 5 to 1.2 hub-marina radii from their leg's ports, so they fit their harbours; MapView._ship_draw_scale()); the Routes switch; Only my routes (off by default; on, the Routes switch draws only this player's own routes, not the other companies', players' or AI rivals', though anchored ships still keep off theirs); Day and night (on by default; off, it's always day: no dark side and no lights, the sun held still over the view's north-west so shadows fall south-east); Birds (on by default; off, no bird is drawn or can be clicked, though their calls still play); and the Tutorial switch in saves started with the tutorial. Audio: sliders 0-100% for Master, Sea and weather (the waves, wind and rain), Wildlife (the animals' calls) and Ship horns (horns and the fog signal), each its own audio bus under Master (made by Settings). The ship look, only my routes, day and night, birds and volumes are this computer's, kept in user://settings.cfg for every game; Routes and the tutorial are the company's, kept in its save.
- Clicking the level (or its bar) opens a popup below it (click again to close): XP into the level and XP needed for the next, the company's XP per minute over the last 10 minutes, and "Coming up": the next level and the next few levels that unlock something (models, "+1 slot: ...", "a new hub"), each with an estimate of how long until you reach it at that rate. Refreshes every second.
- Clicking the money opens the Finances screen.

### 1. World (map) screen
- Turn the globe (drag) and zoom (mouse wheel) supported (see World).
- Ports are clickable circles (sized by the port's size); ships are clickable top-down ship shapes moving along their routes.
- The map's top-right corner holds the Prices picker (and Unlock a region over it while one can be unlocked), with no panel round them; the Routes and Tutorial switches are on the Settings page (below). The "Routes" switch (on by default, saved with the game) draws every sea lane the fleet uses as a faint line in the color of a ship using it: each cargo ship's route loop, any new route waiting to replace it, and the leg it's sailing now. A lane shared by several ships is drawn once. Where a port's marina shows, each line leaves the lane and curves round to the harbour mouth just as the ships do (map_view _lane_curve(), the same curve as _harbour_pose()), keeping to the right-hand side of the entrance, and runs on in through the entrance, stopping where ships part for their berths; a lane sailed both ways shows both ways' curves (in on one side, out on the other) joining one line at sea. Ore carriers' lanes are whitened (their gray colors 65% towards white) and drawn stronger (70% opaque, others 45%), so they show on the dark deep sea. In multiplayer, other players' lanes are drawn the same way underneath, each company's in its own color (dark ones lifted toward white so they show), 25% opaque. Lanes are drawn, and the ships on them placed, just as the lanes are laid (map_view _bent_lane() gives the passes each lane's points and miles as they are). Until 2026-10-13 each lane was bent out round the harbours of the ports it passed but wasn't sailing to (a keep-out zone 1.8 times each port's harbour mouth's distance from it, the lane pushed smoothly out of it, no further than the sea went), to keep the lines and ships out of other marinas and to leave quiet water for ships at anchor; that made the routes swerve and kink round every port on their way, and the ships lie in port now (see Long-term moorings), so it was taken out.
- Only one popup open at a time; opening a new one closes the old one.

### 2. Ships screen
- Scrolling list grouped by model (in shop order), each group on a faint tint of its ship line's color (the same colors as the Prices menu and Finances) under a picture of the model (bow to the right) and its name in that color, 8 compact tiles wide, one tile per owned ship. Groups with no ships (or none matching the filter) are hidden.
- Each tile has its captain's face (a small round picture) beside three lines: the ship name (cut short with "..." if it doesn't fit), then its cargo and level in smaller text ("Coffee · Lv 7", in green with "+2" while it has upgrade points to spend; recovery boats say "Recovery"). A ship that needs the player for anything but upgrade points says why instead, in red ("Held in port", "Paused", "No route", "Lost at sea", "Waiting for toll money"). Then "Capt. " and its captain's name. Hovering a tile shows the full name and its status line (e.g. "Docked at Korsakov — not enough fuel for Hong Kong; turn on refuel").
- Each tile shows the ship name, a status dot, and thin maintenance / fuel / cargo bars. Green = running (has route, not paused, not held); amber = waiting in port for money for a full load; red = docked (paused at a port, no route, or held in port).
- Clicking a tile opens the ship popup.
- Sort (within each model, left to right) by earnings (the default: each ship's profit over the last 30 days of game time (GameState.SHIP_EARNINGS_DAYS: 36 minutes of play at 20 calendar minutes a second; its own window, the rest of Finances keeps to 10 minutes of play), best first, re-sorted every 30 seconds and each time the tab opens; each tile shows its figure along its bottom, green or red, with how long that is in game time, "+$1.2M in 30d" (GameState.recent_window_short(); the tooltip and the Sort menu say it in full)), name, status (lost, then at risk, then held, then stopped, then running) or profit all time; show all ships, running, stopped, lost, or recovery boats. A count shows how many are shown. Tiles re-sort when the fleet or the sort changes, not live.
- An "Upgrade all (N): $X" button (N unspent points, $X what they'd all cost) buys the fleet's upgrades cheapest first, as far as the money goes without dipping into the bank reserve. Each ship's points go round-robin to its lowest skill path, ties in the order speed, efficiency, durability (so speed 1, efficiency 1, durability 1, speed 2, ... up to all 30), which is also its cheapest. Red dot while at least the cheapest is affordable; greyed out (tooltip: the cheapest's price) when there's nothing to buy or it can't be afforded. Each model's header also has its own "Upgrade N: $X" button, left of its Mute, for just that model's ships (hidden while they have none to spend; greyed while the cheapest would dip into the bank reserve).
- Mega upgrade: once the company owns all 8 of a model and every one is at level 30, a "Mega upgrade: $1,000,000" button appears to the right of the model's name (red dot when affordable; greyed, with the reason, when not). It costs 80x the model's price (Scooter 20 $1,000,000, Feeder 100 $10,000,000, Feedermax 200 $40,000,000...; game_config mega) and can be bought once per model. Every ship of that model, including later ones, then gets +50% top speed and +50% profit on each profitable trade (on top of other bonuses); recovery boats get +50% top speed and burn 50% less fuel instead (Buffalo $100,000,000, Mammoth $800,000,000). The name then shows a gold "★ Mega: +50% speed and profit" (or "+50% speed, -50% fuel") badge, and the activity log announces it. Mega upgrades are a line in the Finances totals and are saved.

### 3. Shop screen
- A section per ship line, laid out in rows with the sections of a row side by side, each as wide as its models, cards widening (up to 175 px) to fill rows with fewer ships (up to 6 cards a row, beside the open activity log at the default window): container ships; ore and grain carriers; livestock carriers and tankers; vehicle carriers and recovery boats; icebreakers and, in a wider section to their right, tugs. A row too wide for the window stacks its sections. Cards are compact (prices of $1M and up shortened: "$15M"), one per ship model, showing its stats, price, and how many are owned ("Owned 2 / 10"). Each section sits on a faint tint of its ship line's color (the same colors as the Ships tab, Finances and the Prices menu). The Tugs section has "Buy all tugs (14)": it fills every free tug slot the money allows, each tug given a suggested name and based at the route port that needs one most.

The biggest model on the map (the Mammoth) is 150 px long and the rest scale by the square root of their map length, so small ships stay a good size: a Scooter 20 is about 72 px (a quarter of a Mammoth's length on the map, half here), a Dominator about 120.

### 4. Route Assignment screen
- Full-screen gray version of the map; no top bar or nav bar. The side panel shows the ship's current route, its range, and the port it starts from.
- Click ports in order to add waypoints; a side list shows ports in order plus estimated travel time and distance for each leg and the full loop. Legs through a canal add the lock time (6 chambers × 3 s through Panama, not counting any wait) and show the toll and XP bonus: "to Balboa · 44 nm · 30 s · Panama Canal: toll $4,200, +50% XP".
- Rules: consecutive waypoints must differ; since routes loop, the last waypoint must also differ from the first. Every leg, including the one back to the start, must be within the ship's range.
- Ports the ship can't reach from the last waypoint (or, before any are added, from the port it's docked at or heading to) are greyed out; clicking one explains why. Out-of-range legs show in red in the side list.
- Buttons: Cancel (always available), Undo last waypoint, Accept (enabled once 2+ waypoints and the route is valid). Each stop in the list has an X on the right that takes it out of the route (and one of a pair of the same port left side by side).
- Assigning a new route to a ship at sea: it finishes its current leg, then follows the new route.
- Assigning a route also un-pauses the ship.
- A ship docked at a port that isn't on its new route first sails to the route's first port (a normal, paid leg).
- Running ships leave a port when their port stop ends (and they have fuel for the next leg); paused or route-less ships stay docked.

- The « button at the top of the log folds it away to a thin strip (» opens it again), giving the screens the room; remembered with the game.
- Each model's group on the Ships screen has a Mute button at the right of its title: a muted model's routine news (its ships loading cargo, selling at a profit, entering canals) is left out of the log; losses, breakdowns, lost ships, level-ups and other trouble still show. "Muted" while on; remembered with the game.

## Divine Actions
- A game mode chosen when a company is founded (New Company's "Divine Actions" switch beside Seasonal closures; saved with the game as GameState.divine, scripts/sim/divine.gd; can't be changed later), unrealistic and unfair by design. Rivals (AI and other players) have the same powers.
- Powers, bought from the Divine panel (the bolt button under the trophy on the World map): each row names the power, its price now and what it does, with Unleash (greyed with why: too dear, on cooldown, Wrath used this year, three storms out). Prices scale with company level (base x (1 + level / 50)), except Wrath, a flat $1,000,000,000. Each has a cooldown in game weeks (a week being 504 s of play). A power with a target closes the panel and has the player click it on the map (a hint at the top says what; Esc cancels): a point at sea, a port, or a rival's ship at sea.
  - Storms, three tiers, called down at the point clicked (Weather.spawn_divine): Squall ($25M base; a gale, 180 nm, 2 days, cooldown 0.2 weeks), Tempest ($200M; a hurricane, typhoon or cyclone by those waters, 300 nm, 7 days, with an eye, closes ports under its core; 0.6 weeks) and Wrath ($1B; the tropical kind, 600 nm, 14 days, power 2: twice any natural storm's strength, so 95% slower and 5x wear at its core at Harsh settings, closing every port it crosses; once a calendar year). A divine storm starts at full strength, holds it most of its life and fades at the end (intensity (1 - (age/life)^4) x power), drifts slowly (5 knots) from where it was called, is named for its tier ("Cyclone Wrath") with "sent by <company>" under it on the map and a "Sent by" row in its popup, and acts from company level 1 even in a game founded without storms (then at Harsh settings). At most 3 of a company's storms are out at once. The sender's own ships suffer in it like anyone's.
  - Fair winds ($100M; no target; a week): the company's whole fleet sails 1.5x faster for a week.
  - Calm seas ($50M; a point): every storm within 500 nm blows out at once (natural or divine).
  - Plague of gulls ($30M; a port; a week): gulls mob the port (drawn wheeling over it); every other company's ships there take twice as long over their stops.
  - Deluge ($80M; a port; a week): rain over the port; its prices halve for everyone (buying and selling).
  - Sea change ($60M; a port; a month): a great flock (drawn as a golden ring of birds) settles on the port: it counts 40 more birds for the port blessing below.
  - The Kraken ($150M; a rival's ship at sea): the ship is lost where it is ("taken by the Kraken", maintenance 0) for a tug or recovery boat to fetch; its arms rise round the ship for 20 s.
  - Lightning strike ($120M; a port where a rival has a hub): the hub drops a level; a bolt and flash over the port for 4 s.
- Blessings and omens, always on in a Divine game (GameState hooks): birds following a ship speed it up 2% a bird (an albatross 4%), up to 50%; whales or dolphins within 50 nm shield a ship from a storm's wear; sharks within 100 nm of a lost ship hurry its rescuers 1.5x; frigatebirds following a ship make off with 2% a bird (up to 10%) of its sale (logged); a port's gulls, cormorants, seabirds and sea ducks (plus a Sea change) bless it, 80 birds for the full blessing: its stops up to 50% faster and its prices up to 20% better each way for ships there; a port's lamps by night turn ships round 20% faster; the first snow (1-7 December, ports north of 50 N) slows stops there 30%; cherry blossom (Japan in April) doubles delivery XP there; and one day a year a comet crosses the sky (drawn above the night at the top of the map, logged) lifting everyone's sale prices 10%.
- Every act goes in the activity log in the actor's colour ("Northstar Freight called down a Tempest on the waters off Lagos."; red when it hits this player's ships, port or hub); the panel lists the last 12 acts. AI rivals use the powers too (RivalAI._divine): about once a game week (Easy every 3, Hard every 1) a rival picks on the richest other company and uses the first power it can afford twice over that it has a target for (a storm or the Kraken on one of that company's ships at sea, a plague or deluge on its busiest port, lightning on one of its hubs, fair winds for itself, calm seas where its own ship is in a storm, a great flock on its own busiest port).
- Multiplayer: the host does every act (Net command divine_act; a ship target goes as its id) and sends the divine state (effects, cooldowns, ledger) with its updates; players can target each other and the AI. Achievements and company comparison are unchanged.


## AI rivals
- Computer-run shipping companies (scripts/sim/rival_ai.gd, RivalAI; Company.ai "easy", "normal" or "hard"; "" for a person's), chosen when founding a game: New Company's "AI rivals" row (under Storms) has a drop-down for how many (None, the default, to 5) and Easy, Normal or Hard for them all (greyed with None; Normal to start with; each explained in its tooltip). They're ordinary companies in the game's list, saved with it like any other (the save keeps each one's difficulty), and carry on from where they were when it's loaded.
- Each gets a name no company has (two words from RivalAI's lists, e.g. "Northstar Freight", no two rivals sharing a first word), a company color no company has (not black, white or gray), a CEO made up at random, the starting money, an HQ at its home port and that port's market region. Home ports come from the recommended starts by difficulty (data/starts.json): Easy at fair or hard starts, Normal at great or fair, Hard at excellent or great; never a port a company has, and in a market region no company is in when there is one.
- They play by the same rules and prices as the player, with no bonuses: they act through the same GameState functions a player's buttons use, as their own company (GameState.ai_acting meanwhile, so none of it goes in this player's log and nothing saves; the next autosave keeps it). The host runs them (or single player), each thinking every few seconds of play (Easy about 15 s, Normal 7 s, Hard 3 s, spread out so they don't think at once). Each think, a rival:
  - Keeps afloat: in debt, it borrows a loan step if the bank will lend, else sells its least useful docked ship (never its last cargo ship); it never goes bankrupt or leaves the game. With money to spare it pays its loans back a step at a time.
  - Opens a market region when its level allows (the one with a port nearest its hubs), and founds a hub when its level allows (of the 30 biggest unlocked ports, the one its dearest cargo model would earn most from; weighed up 4 ports a think so the game never stalls on it).
  - Buys ships: of the cargo models it can buy (unlocked, a slot free), the one that would pay for itself soonest on the best loop it can find from one of its hubs, within its difficulty's payback time (Easy 15 min, Normal 25, Hard 40, up to 4x that when it has plenty of money for the price; any ship that makes money while it has fewer than 3), keeping back half a load's money for each of its cargo ships (and Easy 30%, Normal 10% of the rest). A Hard rival buys up to 3 a think, and borrows for a ship that pays back in half its time (up to a loan step for every 5 company levels, and one to start). Each new ship sets off at once on its loop, with auto-recover on. A loop is 2 ports (and 3 for Normal and Hard), every leg one the model can sail between ports it has unlocked, judged by $ a second: each leg's best cargo at its own prices (sale bonuses, less the port fee away from its hubs and canal tolls), less fuel, repairs and crew wages, over sailing, port, lock and convoy time. A loop its other ships already sail counts 20% less for each, so its fleet spreads out. Easy picks one of its best 5 loops at random, Normal one of its best 2, Hard the best.
  - Gives any cargo ship without a route the best loop from where it is; Normal (every 5 minutes) and Hard (every 2) look at one of their ships and move it to a loop at least 25% better.
  - Spends (Easy every 4th think, Normal and Hard every 2nd): ships' upgrade points (Upgrade all), hub upgrade points (Sale prices, then Port stops, Fuel & repairs, Ship XP), mega upgrades it qualifies for, and industry buildings on its hubs' open plots (the commodity its ships trade most there). A hub upgrade, industry building or mega upgrade must cost no more than 10% (Easy), 15% (Normal) or 25% (Hard) of its money.
  - Buys service ships (every 3rd think): tugs once it has 4 cargo ships (up to 2, and one more for every 4 cargo ships), a recovery boat once it has 5 (the cheapest), and with seasonal closures an icebreaker (the cheapest that can work it) for each icebreaker area its routes' ports are in.
  - Saves up: when the ship it wants costs more than it has, its bank reserve keeps half its money back for it; otherwise its reserve is nothing.
- Speed: what it works out is kept (its loops for 20-40 s of play, the ports a model can sail to from a port for 5 minutes, its fleet's counts for the think), at most 2 sets of loops are worked out afresh a think (the rest wait for a later one), and it looks at service ships every 3rd think: 3 Hard rivals take about 12% of the simulation's time over the first 40 minutes (about 5 s of 38). A rival's very first think in a session takes about 0.7 s while the sea lanes it looks at are first read in.
- What the player sees: rivals' ships sail the map (fainter than the player's, like other players' in multiplayer; their popups are read-only, "— Northstar Freight's ship"), their HQ and hubs have rings in their color, and they're in Finances → Compare companies marked "(AI · Hard)". Their big moves go in the activity log in their color: "Northstar Freight launched its first Feeder 100.", opened up a region, founded a hub, built an industry building, mega-upgraded a model, bought its first recovery boat, stationed an icebreaker, and every 10 company levels.
- The world's own features (storms, seasonal closures, ports shrinking late on) start by the highest level of the people playing (GameState.world_level()): rivals' levels don't count.
- Multiplayer: the host picks rivals when founding the game (the same New Company screen) and the host's game runs them; joined players see them like any company, and can't take one over (they're shown as taken and refused).

## Multiplayer
- Like CK3: one player hosts a game from one of their Multiplayer saves and friends join it, each running their own company in the same world. The economy is shared (one set of markets that everyone's trades move, port sizes, storms, seasons and calendar). Every company has its own home port. Only the host sets the game speed (joined players' speed buttons are greyed out, showing the host's, and F does nothing for them).
- Hosting: Multiplayer page → pick a slot (New Game to host, or a save) → Host Game dialog: the game's name in friends' lists ("Name your game"; the company's if left blank), an optional password, how many can play (2 to 8, the host included), and Over: Steam (the default when Steam is running) or Local network, or by address, and Filter: "Filter bad words from ship names and chat" (on by default). With it on, the host blanks inappropriate words out with asterisks (scripts/util/word_filter.gd, words in data/word_filter.json): in every chat line before it goes out, in every ship name bought or renamed by any player, in the game's name, and in the ship names already in the save when hosting starts. It reads through simple disguises (sh1t, f.u.c.k, f u c k, fuuuck) without catching place names (Scunthorpe, Fukuoka, Essex). The games list says "Filtered" for such games. The game then opens as usual and is open to join.
- Over Steam (GodotSteam, addons/godotsteam; Windows, macOS and Linux 64-bit): the game starts Steam as app 480, Valve's test app, until it has its own app id. Hosting opens a public Steam lobby holding the game's details (game "maritime_empire", version, name, host company, players, max players, password or not), and joined games connect to the host through Steam's relay, so no addresses or port forwarding. The host's players list has Invite Friends (Steam's overlay; it only opens when Steam started the game, otherwise it says so and friends use the games list). Accepting a Steam invite, or Join Game on a friend in Steam's friends list, opens the join page and joins that game (while playing, the activity log says to leave this game first; back at the title screen it joins). Without Steam running, the Over choice is greyed out with the reason.
- Over the local network: Godot's ENet, on port 24565 (UDP); the game announces itself on the local network every second on port 24566.
- Joining: Multiplayer page → Join a Friend's Game: your name (your Steam name to start with), then the games hosted on Steam and on your network ("Rory's Empire · Host Co · 1/4 players · Password · Filtered · Steam", with Join; Steam's list is asked again every 5 seconds and leaves out your own account's games), or join by address (an IP address and port; over the internet the host forwards port 24565). A game with a password asks for it; a wrong one, a full game or a different version of the game is turned away with the reason. Then pick a company: take back one of the game's companies no one is playing (with its level, money and home port; not an AI rival), or found a new one (name, color, and its HQ picked on a globe the same way as New Company: only the recommended starts and the Great Lakes ports no company has, colored by kind, with the start's note; the first free recommended start is picked to start with), then "Next: Your CEO": the same CEO page as a new game's (scripts/ui/ceo_editor.gd), with Back or Found Company and Join; the host takes the CEO as sent, made safe (Characters.clean(): name trimmed, each part within its options; one made up at random if it has no name). It starts with its home port's region unlocked, like a new game (saves where a joined company had none get it, and every region its fleet uses, when loaded). Founding at a taken home port, a port that isn't a starting port, or with a taken name says so and lets you pick again.
- While playing: the host's game runs the world as in single player. About seven times a second it sends each joined game what changed (the clock, speed, weather, market and each company's money and XP every time; each moving ship's position, fuel, maintenance and timers; any ship whose state changed whole; and the rest of every ship, the port sizes and the companies' finances at least every 3 seconds), and the joined games move ships smoothly in between at their last speeds. A joined player's actions (buy, sell, routes, pause, the toggles, upgrades, hubs, mega upgrades, recoveries, icebreaker stations, renames, the bank reserve, their map and log settings) go to the host, which does them for that player's company (only for their own ships); what happens to their ships (sales, loads, breakdowns, losses, rescues, level-ups, canal tolls, port growth) comes back as their own activity log messages.
- Other players' ships show on the map a little faded, with a flag in their company's color at the stern; their HQs and hubs have rings in their company's color (companies sharing a port ring it in turn). Clicking another player's ship shows it read-only ("... — Host Co's ship"), without its money or controls. Their route lanes show faintly in their company's color (see the Routes switch). The Ships, Finances, Hubs and Shop screens, the lost and at-risk markers and the alerts are your own company's.
- The top bar shows "Hosting · 3 players" or "Joined on Steam · 3 players" (the tooltip names them); clicking it lists the players with Leave Game (and Invite Friends for a host on Steam) (the host saves and ends the game for everyone; a joined player leaves and their company carries on, to be taken back by joining again). The activity panel has two tabs, Activity and Chat: Chat lists the game's messages ("Company: text" in the speaker's company color, newest at the bottom; the last 200) with a "Say something (Enter)" line under them, and the tab counts messages that came while it wasn't open ("Chat (2)"). T opens the panel on the Chat tab ready to type (not while typing elsewhere); Esc leaves the chat line. Joins and leaves are logged in the host's Activity. If the host leaves or the connection drops, joined players are told so and go back to the title screen.
- The host's save holds every company (each ship's company, and which company is the host's); joined games don't save. Storms, seasonal closures and port shrinking follow the highest company level in the game; breakdowns start at each company's own level; rescues use each company's own recovery boats and tugs.
- Under the hood: Company (scripts/sim/company.gd) holds everything a player owns; GameState.company is this player's, and the screens use GameState's money, hubs... (that company's), while the sim pays, charges and levels each ship's own company. Net (scripts/autoload/net.gd) does the hosting, joining, updates, commands, events, chat and finding games, over Steam (SteamMultiplayerPeer) or ENet; past connecting, both are the same. The whole game a newcomer starts from goes in parts of at most 256 KB (Steam won't send a message over 512 KB).
